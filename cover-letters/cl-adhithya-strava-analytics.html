Hiring Team, Strava

Brand and growth measurement at consumer scale almost always breaks at the same place: the team is comparing apples (incrementality lift) to oranges (MTA) without an explicit hierarchy that tells each method what it is allowed to claim. Lift is causal at the program level. MMM is allocational across channels and time. MTA is operational inside the funnel. The fix is rarely a better model; it is a methodology hierarchy that gets written down before the disputes start.

I have built that thinking three different ways. At Plug Power I rebuilt closed-loop attribution from ad click to SQL on Salesforce, Adobe Analytics, GA4, and GTM. UTM spec from scratch, GTM containers rebuilt, lead routing validated through Salesforce campaign attribution. CPA dropped 25%, conversion went up 20%. The plumbing was the unlock; once the data was clean, every test compounded.

At Pixis I validated a 3x ROAS lift for a fashion partner using holdout incrementality testing and reconciled it against creative-variant diagnostics that aggregate ROAS would have hidden. I also designed measurement playbooks for an 8-client B2B SaaS portfolio on a $1.4M budget: metric definitions, lift-test cadence, reporting standards, all documented before the disputes started.

At Sensata I built a $627M margin-recovery executive Power BI dashboard tracking 1,200 opportunities across three regions, four product families, and 20 product lines. Senior leadership reads it weekly. The same approach maps to Strava brand-and-growth measurement: classify, track velocity, surface what crosses the threshold for action.
