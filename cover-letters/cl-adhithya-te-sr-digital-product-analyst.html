Hiring Team, Digital Center of Excellence — TE Connectivity

Enterprise digital-platform projects fail at the definition stage, not the implementation stage. The CMS, the PIM, the eCommerce tooling — those are choices that can be corrected later. What can't be corrected later is three different business teams quietly using the same word to mean three different things, signing off on the JIRA tickets anyway, and ending up with a platform that works exactly as specified and solves nothing.

At Plug Power, I watched this almost happen. Sales, marketing, and ops all agreed they wanted a “lead” tracked end-to-end in Salesforce. When I sat down to actually configure the workflows, it turned out sales meant “someone who took a sales call,” marketing meant “anyone who filled a form,” and ops meant “anyone with a valid email.” I spent two weeks doing nothing but calibrating that definition across teams before writing a single rule. Then I stood up the full stack — Salesforce, Adobe Analytics, GA, GTM — and the whole thing worked because the definitions were right. -25% CPA came from the clean data, not the creative.

That's the instinct I'd bring to the Digital COE:

Agile documentation as a discipline, not a ceremony. At Sensata I've managed a 100+ feature backlog across 3 regions for 18 months — user stories, acceptance criteria, dependency maps, release notes. The hardest part isn't writing them. It's refusing to let "we'll figure out the acceptance criteria later" into the queue. I don't. That's why we cut TTM 20%.

Measurement on the work the platform enables, not just the platform. Dashboards that never change get ignored. At Sensata I built a SQL + GA4 framework on 50K+ connected devices and put it on a monthly review cadence with product and engineering. Feature adoption lifted 18%. The framework mattered less than the fact that leadership actually used it to reprioritize.

Adobe Analytics is not just a skill on a resume for me. I configured it at Plug Power end-to-end — tagging, event taxonomy, segmentation, attribution against the Salesforce pipeline. I've read the docs on PIM/DAM integration patterns, but my hands-on is on the martech side, not the PIM side. Your preferred-quals explicitly value PIM experience, and I want to be straight: I'd be ramping on PIM-specific governance in the first 90 days. I'd do it fast, but I wouldn't pretend otherwise.

The catch is that I'd want the first ~30 days to be heavily synchronous with the COE team so I can see how the platform decisions actually get made, who owns what, and where the unwritten tradeoffs live. Platforms are political. Remote-first makes that harder to read, not easier.
