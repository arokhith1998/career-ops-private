Business Banking is the rare digital marketing book where the buyer actually reads the email. Small-business owners and middle-market CFOs are time-poor, ROI-driven, and genuinely making the buying decision themselves. That changes the work. Attribution is cleaner because the buyer clicks through with intent. Creative has to be specific because they will skim past anything that sounds like a brand campaign. The execution craft your JD lists, owned and earned and paid, plus website creative, plus email, plus measurement, is the part of digital marketing that actually moves a number with that buyer. It is the part of the work I want to do.

I have done this work in three places. At Plug Power I rebuilt the B2B paid stack across Google, Meta, LinkedIn, and programmatic display, while writing the UTM spec, rebuilding GTM containers, and stitching closed-loop attribution from ad click through Salesforce campaigns to SQL. CPA dropped 25%, conversion went up 20%, and we could finally tell a CFO which dollar bought which lead. At Pixis I ran an 8-client B2B SaaS portfolio on a $1.4M budget, with weekly multi-channel optimization and per-client playbooks tied to renewal criteria. Churn down 50%. At GenY I rebuilt a D2C e-commerce paid stack to 16x ROAS by fixing the Shopping feed and structured campaigns nobody had touched in a year.

On the website and email side, I built SwipeHire end-to-end: CMS and landing pages on Python and React, GA4 + GTM event tracking from day one, eight A/B tests on activation flows, and an integrated email-plus-paid nurture cycle. 50+ users, +25% activation. The instrumentation came before the launch, which is what made the experiments useful instead of decorative.
