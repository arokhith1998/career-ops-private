Marketing analytics for a consumer subscription app comes down to one question: which behavior in week one survives to month three. Activation experiments either build the early habit that converts trial to paid and paid to retained, or they decorate the funnel and make the dashboard look better while LTV decays. The teams that win instrument the right cohort cuts before the test ships and treat creative-variant diagnostics as more important than aggregate ROAS, because aggregate metrics hide the signal that actually moves retention.

I have done this work three different ways. At Plug Power I rebuilt closed-loop attribution from ad click to SQL on Salesforce, Adobe Analytics, GA4, and GTM. UTM spec from scratch, GTM containers rebuilt, lead routing validated through Salesforce campaign attribution. CPA dropped 25%, conversion went up 20%. The plumbing was the unlock. Once the data was clean, every experiment compounded.

At Pixis I validated a 3x ROAS lift for a fashion partner via holdout incrementality testing and reconciled the lift against creative-variant diagnostics that aggregate ROAS would have hidden. I also designed measurement playbooks for an 8-client B2B SaaS portfolio on a $1.4M budget. Metric definitions, lift-test cadence, and reporting standards documented in writing so methodology arguments stopped happening on Slack.

SwipeHire is the consumer-app analog. Built solo on Python, OpenAI API, and React. Instrumented GA4 plus GTM event tracking from day one, ran eight A/B tests on activation flows, and kept a weekly retention cohort cadence. 50+ users, +25% activation. The instrumentation came before the launch, which is what made the experiments useful instead of decorative.
