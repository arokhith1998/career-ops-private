Cross-functional program work breaks at one specific seam: the gap between the strategy deck and the operating cadence that makes the strategy real. Most "we missed our roadmap" stories are not strategy failures. They are operating-rhythm failures, where the weekly check-ins do not exist, the escalation thresholds were never written down, and the tradeoff conversation happens for the first time the week before launch. The teams that ship on time built the operating rhythm before the strategy needed it.

I have done that work under different titles for four years. At Sensata I lead a $627M margin-recovery program tracking 1,200 opportunities across three regions, four product families, and twenty product lines. I designed and present the executive Power BI dashboard senior leadership reads weekly. The dashboard is the strategy artifact; the weekly review is the operating rhythm. Both have to exist for either to work. I also own a 100+ feature Agile backlog with engineering and regional sales across three regions, and I have cut time-to-market 20% by disciplined sprint cadence and tradeoff facilitation, not by working harder.

At Plug Power I ran a 4-vendor MarTech RFP and rollout: scoped requirements, scored vendors against attribution depth and integration cost, negotiated terms with Procurement, aligned six internal stakeholders across sales, ops, and engineering. I documented the SLAs and escalation paths in writing so issues had a track record before they became problems. The rollout shipped on time and cut CPA 25% with conversion up 20%, but the more durable outcome was the reusable operating templates the next program manager inherited.

At Pixis I led an 8-person team across creative, account management, and platform specialists, and managed an 8-client B2B SaaS portfolio on a $1.4M budget. Held vendors and clients to delivery standards in writing. Replaced two channels mid-quarter when KPIs slipped. Churn dropped 50% through per-client playbooks tied to renewal criteria. Multi-stakeholder, escalation-heavy operating work at portfolio scale.

And during my MS at Simon Business School, I led a five-consultant pro-bono engagement for Safran. TAM and SAM across 170 facilities, GTM entry-options analysis, pricing and partnership recommendations presented to Safran leadership. That is the closest thing on my CV to consulting tenure.

I am honest that my titles say Pricing Lead and Customer Success Manager, not Program Manager. The scope of the work is what makes it relevant to your team. Brooklyn hybrid is fine, NYC is my secondary preferred metro. Happy to walk through any of the above in more detail.
