Most "vendor problems" in paid demand are scope problems someone forgot to write down. The reason third-party syndication blows up six weeks before a renewal is almost never that the vendor is bad. It is that the SLA, the suppression standard, the lead-format spec, and the escalation path were never in the same document. The way you have framed this role, governance and net-new VP+ contacts as the headline, says you already know that.

I have run the buyer side of this exact relationship at two different companies. At Plug Power I built the closed-loop attribution that 3P vendors deliver into: I wrote the UTM spec, rebuilt GTM containers, and validated lead routing all the way through Salesforce campaign attribution. CPA dropped 25%, conversion went up 20%, and for the first time the team could tell a CFO which dollar bought which SQL. The MarTech plumbing was the unlock. Most "attribution" failures are tagging failures. Fix the plumbing first.

At Pixis I held vendors and clients to delivery, suppression, and lead-quality standards across an 8-client, $1.4M book. We ran weekly performance reviews against written SLAs, escalated underperformers, and replaced two channels mid-quarter when their numbers slipped. Churn fell 50%. The lesson I took out of that book of business: vendors and clients respect a one-page SLA more than a 30-slide QBR. Write it down before there is a problem and the conversation gets a lot shorter when there is.

What I have not done yet is run TechTarget, BrightTALK, or Madison Logic specifically. I have run the operating model. The named vendors are a week-one ramp.

One more thing on the JD's net-new VP+ language. Net-new is not just a top-of-funnel question. The suppression rules at the bottom of the funnel decide whether your "net-new" is real. I rewrote those rules at Plug Power. I would expect to do the same here on day one and use that to set the baseline for what good looks like with each vendor.
