Paid Social for a D2C baby brand is the cleanest test bed in performance marketing. The buyer (a sleep-deprived new parent at 11pm scrolling Instagram) arrives with high intent, the conversion event is real (purchase, not lead), the funnel is short enough to read week-over-week, and creative fatigue lives in days rather than quarters. The teams that win are the ones that ship the next creative variant before the current one fatigues, and the ones that built the attribution plumbing so they actually know which variant did the work.

I have run that exact mechanic at GenY. D2C consumer eCommerce brand, full Meta plus Search plus Shopping plus Display, Google Merchant Center and Meta catalog rebuild from the feed up. Feed cleanup, schema fixes, metadata rewrite, structured Shopping and Advantage+ campaigns, weekly creative refresh tested against fatigue. ROAS climbed to 16x, sustained through the catalog rebuild and the creative cadence. The mechanic transfers directly to Newton Baby: feed integrity is the foundation, structured campaigns are the engine, creative refresh is the multiplier.

At Pixis I ran an 8-client D2C and e-commerce portfolio on a $1.4M media budget. Meta and Google and programmatic and email, weekly multi-channel optimization. For a leading fashion D2C partner, I delivered 3x ROAS in 30 days through full-funnel restructuring (Shopping feed, smart bidding, creative refresh, lookalike expansion) and validated the lift via holdout incrementality testing. Aggregate ROAS would have shown the same number; the holdout proved the lift was causal rather than seasonal. That same testing rigor is what I would bring to Newton Baby's Meta program.

On the attribution side, I rebuilt closed-loop attribution at Plug Power on Salesforce, Adobe Analytics, GA4, and GTM. UTM spec from scratch, GTM containers rebuilt, lead routing validated through Salesforce campaign attribution. CPA dropped 25%, conversion went up 20%. The plumbing was the unlock; once the data was clean, every test compounded.

On the experimentation muscle, SwipeHire is the proof. I shipped it solo on Python, React, and OpenAI API with GA4 plus GTM event tracking from day one. Eight A/B tests on activation flows and Meta-ads-adjacent landing pages, weekly retention cohort analysis, +25% activation. Building the instrumentation before the launch is what made the experiments useful instead of decorative.

What I have not used directly is Shopify as a named platform. The catalog and feed-management work I did at GenY (Google Merchant Center, Meta catalog) is conceptually identical, and Shopify is a week-one ramp.

Remote (USA) works. Happy to walk through any of the above in more detail.
