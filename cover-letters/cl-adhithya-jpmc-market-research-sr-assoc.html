Most market research investments fail in the brief, not the fieldwork. By the time the data comes back from the vendor, the team has already moved on, or three stakeholders quietly meant three different things by "purchase intent," or the original decision the research was meant to inform got made in a hallway six weeks ago. The interesting craft of senior research is not just methodology. It is forcing the alignment conversation upfront, naming what decision the research is going to drive, and refusing to start fieldwork until the answer to that question is unambiguous.

That is the part of this role I want to work on, and it lines up with what I have actually done.

At Sensata I ran 30+ structured interviews with OEMs and distributors across NA and EU, and a separate competitive analysis of 10+ industrial-sensor players. The hard part was not the interviewing. It was getting product, sales, pricing, and 3 regional GMs to agree on what specifically we were trying to answer before I drafted a single guide. I spent the first three weeks just running pre-research alignment workshops. The interviews then took 5 weeks and went smoothly, because everyone already knew what would and would not move. CSAT lifted 22%, market share lifted 8%, and the recommendations got implemented without re-debate. The discipline was upfront, not downstream.

Three other things from the JD that line up directly:

Mixed-method synthesis at scale. The Bleeders and Leakers project I built is a 4-page Power BI dashboard analyzing 1,200 pricing opportunities worth $627M across 3 regions and 20 product lines. It exposed $433M in margin gaps and informed leadership prioritization down to the salesperson and product family level. Quantitative research lands when you start by writing the executive summary first, then build the data to support or contradict it.

Stakeholder management for senior leadership. I built 3-scenario pricing models with sensitivity analysis for Sensata's AP26 planning cycle and presented them to senior executives. Executives do not want data. They want a decision framed by data. I lead with the recommendation, walk back to the evidence, and leave the model open in case they want to push on it.

Project ownership in ambiguity. I led a 5-person consulting team for Safran (B2B aerospace and defense) on a Simon pro-bono engagement: TAM and SAM across 170 facilities, 4-vertical persona development, positioning for 3 product lines, and a final presentation to Safran leadership. The brief was loose. We tightened it ourselves before doing any analysis. That is the same muscle this role describes when it asks for "structure and direction out of ambiguity."

One honest note: I have not worked in financial services. Closest analogs are industrial B2B (Sensata) and AI SaaS (Pixis), both stakeholder-heavy enterprise environments where research had to hold up under exec scrutiny. I would expect the first 30 days to be deep on JPMC's research stack and consumer segments before I propose anything new.
