Partnership marketing usually breaks at one specific place, and it is almost never the place people talk about. It is not the creative. It is not the campaign channel. It is the moment, six weeks before launch, when the partner says "we want to measure awareness" and your team needs pipeline. If that conversation happens after the campaign is built, the partnership is already fraying. If it happens before, you have a real program.

At Sensata, I ran this exact conversation over and over with OEMs and distributors. Every partner brought their own definition of success: distributor reach, OEM spec-in rate, joint case studies, leads. I did 30+ interviews, found where their goals and ours actually overlapped, and co-built the enablement assets (decks, battlecards, case studies) around that Venn. 50+ reps used them. Partner win rates went up 30%. The content worked because partners helped build it, which meant they actually used it, and because we agreed on the metric math before anyone signed off on a campaign.

I do not have to translate between marketer and engineer. I have been both.

Partner portfolio management at platform scale. At Pixis I managed an 8-client B2B SaaS portfolio with $1.4M in budget across global D2C and e-commerce partners. Co-marketing, joint campaigns, weekly performance reviews. Client churn dropped 50%. Partnership marketing at platform scale is the same craft: understand where each partner's goals and yours overlap, kill the rest, stay disciplined about shared metrics.
