Paid social for ticketing breaks Meta's default playbook in two specific ways, and I think that's the most interesting part of this role.

First: demand spikes are violent. A tour announcement, a playoff run, a surprise Super Bowl matchup — demand jumps 10-50x in hours. Meta's optimization algorithms lag this, because they're trained on recent historical conversion patterns. If you don't manually intervene — budget floors, bid caps off, expanded creative variants pre-loaded — you watch a buying window close while the algorithm is still "learning." Second: inventory is ephemeral. Once the event starts, unsold tickets are worth zero. So the cost function is fundamentally different from e-commerce — you're not optimizing for lifetime value, you're optimizing for time-to-fill. Those two facts reshape almost every decision: creative cadence, bid strategy, audience expansion, and what "good" looks like on a dashboard.

I haven't run paid social for ticketing specifically. But I've run the closest adjacencies — high-velocity e-commerce with aggressive seasonal spikes — and the intuitions travel.

Demand-spike handling at Pixis. I ran a $1.4M budget across a portfolio of global D2C and e-commerce brands on Meta, Google, and programmatic. One fashion partner went from flat ROAS to 3x in 30 days after a full-funnel rebuild: Shopping feed cleanup, creative refresh, smart bidding layered on top of lookalikes, and critically — holdout incrementality tests so we could prove the lift was real and not just pulled from organic. That incrementality discipline is how I'd approach StubHub: during a spike, pulling budget feels like magic until you run the holdout and realize 40% of it was going to happen anyway.

Catalog + Advantage+ at GenY. Delivered 16x ROAS for a D2C brand by rebuilding the Meta catalog from the ground up, launching Advantage+ with creative variants keyed to SKUs, and owning the seasonal promotion calendar end-to-end. Ticketing catalogs (events, sections, price tiers) have similar data-hygiene problems — a dirty catalog caps performance regardless of budget.

StubHub's audience is consumer-broad, so Meta creative breadth is the right primary lever.
