The hardest part of advertiser-facing measurement is not the math. It is that MMM, MTA, and lift testing answer different questions and have to be reconciled at the right scope. Lift is causal at the program level. MMM is allocational across channels and time. MTA is operational inside the funnel. When advertisers say "your numbers do not match mine," they are usually comparing apples (your incrementality lift) to oranges (their MTA). The answer is not a better model; it is a methodology hierarchy that tells each method what it is allowed to claim. That is the framework I want to build for OpenAI Ads.

My measurement substance comes from three places. At Plug Power I rebuilt closed-loop attribution from ad click to SQL on Salesforce, Adobe Analytics, GA4, and GTM. I wrote the UTM spec, rebuilt GTM containers, validated routing into Salesforce campaign attribution, and designed suppression-list logic for net-new evaluation. CPA dropped 25%, conversion went up 20%, and the team could finally compare creative-variant outcomes apples-to-apples. The plumbing was the unlock.

At Pixis I validated a 3x ROAS lift for a fashion partner using holdout incrementality testing. The aggregate ROAS would have shown the same lift; the holdout proved the lift was causal rather than seasonal. I also designed measurement playbooks for an 8-client B2B SaaS portfolio on a $1.4M budget: metric definitions, lift-test cadence, reporting standards, all documented in writing so methodology arguments stopped happening on Slack.

At Sensata I built a $627M margin-recovery executive Power BI dashboard tracking 1,200 opportunities across three regions, four product families, and 20 product lines. Bleeders versus leakers framing, regional scorecards, win-rate analytics. Senior-stakeholder reporting at scale. And outside the corporate work, I built an R-based pricing prediction model using regression for demand elasticity, which is the econometric muscle MMM uses.

The credentialing anchor: MS in Marketing Analytics from Simon Business School (University of Rochester). Pricing Analytics, Advanced Pricing, Data Management and Visualization, Consumer Behavior. The quantitative foundation matches the role.
