Hiring Team, Worldwide Grocery Stores — Amazon

Grocery has a problem most marketing analytics orgs aren't actually set up to solve: the cadence is baked in. People buy groceries every week whether you market to them or not. So the interesting question isn't "did the email work" — it's "how much of what they'd have bought anyway did marketing actually cause." That's not an attribution question. It's an incrementality question, and it requires a measurement framework designed for it from the start.

That's the part of this role I want to work on.

At Sensata, I built a measurement framework in SQL and GA4 on behavioral data from 50K+ connected devices. No one asked me to; the team was using a stack of one-off reports and disagreeing about what "adoption" meant. I defined the KPI taxonomy, instrumented cohorts, automated the dashboards, and spent the first three months arguing about definitions with product and engineering. Feature adoption lifted 18%, but the bigger win was that leadership stopped debating the numbers in meetings. That's the framework-first instinct I'd bring to Whole Foods + Fresh.

At Pixis I ran the other half of this job — the leadership half. 8-person team, $1.4M in budgets, a portfolio of global D2C and e-commerce clients. Weekly exec dashboards tracking ROAS and iROAS. I wasn't just pulling data; I was calibrating what our analysts were allowed to claim, building vertical-specific playbooks, and protecting their deep-work time from the meeting churn that actually drove attrition. Client churn dropped 50%. I learned that managing analysts is 80% clear priorities and 20% defending them.

Two things I want to name directly because your JD does:

Marketing mix modeling / MTA. I haven't run a full MMM production system. I've run the adjacent work — full-funnel attribution at Plug Power (-25% CPA), incrementality-aware A/B tests at Sensata, iROAS holdouts at Pixis. I understand the tradeoffs between bottom-up MTA and top-down MMM and why grocery probably wants both: MMM for upper-funnel channels where attribution is weak, MTA for digital where signal is clean. I'd ramp fast; I wouldn't fake it.

Whole Foods and Fresh are different buyers. One framework across both is hard — basket composition, frequency, and brand intent diverge. I'd push to keep a shared metrics layer (spend efficiency, incremental lift) and split the buyer-journey layer (acquisition cadence, cross-shop behavior) from day one rather than letting it calcify into two separate orgs.
