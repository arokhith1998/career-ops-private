Why this deck exists

North America Marketing is three businesses, Consumer, B2B and Marketing Services, competing for one investment budget.

What a three-business portfolio runs into

Consumer, B2B and Marketing Services each generate their own initiatives, forecasts and asks. Without one holistic view, the loudest initiative wins the budget instead of the strongest one.
Annual and multi-year planning cycles need a repeatable way to prioritize investment, not a fresh argument every cycle between three teams with three different scorecards.
The fix is not one great analysis. It is a standing way to size the opportunity, rank it against the others, and turn that ranking into a business case leadership can act on fast.

What I have already run, in two other industries

At Sensata, I built the FY2026 Bleeders and Leakers dashboard: 1,200 pricing opportunities ranked against a $627M target across 3 regions and 4 product families, with $433M in margin gaps surfaced for leadership to prioritize.
At Safran, I led a 5-consultant team that sized 170 facilities into a SAM of 28 across 4 verticals, then presented the market-entry case directly to leadership for a go or no-go call.
I am running the same discipline now at Applied Materials, turning fragmented Sales, Engineering and Accounts input into one market view, with recurring analysis automated through Claude AI agents.
A Manager who maintains a comprehensive view of the Marketing portfolio and helps leadership prioritize investment across three businesses is describing this exact loop: size the opportunity, rank it, build the case, repeat it every planning cycle.

First 30 days

What the first month on Marketing Strategy & Portfolio Planning looks like.

Week 1
Map the current portfolio
Pull the existing view of Consumer, B2B and Marketing Services initiatives, investment allocation and expected outcomes. Sit with the Strategy, Planning and Operations team to hear which analyses actually moved an investment decision last cycle.
Why first: the strongest prioritization framework is usually visible in what already worked last cycle, the same move that opened the Sensata dashboard build.
Week 2-3
Build the prioritization framework
Turn the strongest patterns into a repeatable scoring and ranking approach across the three businesses, then pressure-test it against a real initiative with the Marketing, Product and Analytics teams.
Why now: pressure-testing against a real initiative, not a hypothetical one, is what made the Sensata dashboard and the Safran case defensible in front of leadership.
Week 4
Support the investment case
Package the ranked portfolio into an executive-ready business case for the next planning cycle, with risks, dependencies and AI-efficiency opportunities flagged for leadership to act on.
Why this fast: a portfolio view only earns its keep once it drives an actual investment call.
