Why this deck exists

Cross Border is not one strategy problem. It is one problem repeated across five regions, each with its own persona.

What a five-region growth pillar runs into

A student traveler in Tokyo and an expat in Madrid do not respond to the same value proposition, the same offer structure or the same issuer partnership. Every corridor and every destination effectively needs its own market case.
Building out new destinations (Madrid, Tokyo, Egypt) while strengthening the ones that already work (Paris, New York) means the strategy team has to run market prioritization and persona work continuously, not once.
The fix is not one great analysis. It is a repeatable way to size a market, benchmark the competitive set, and turn both into a business case a regional team can act on fast.

What I have already run, in two other industries

At Safran, I led a 5-consultant team that sized 170 facilities down to a SAM of 28 and built personas across 4 verticals, each with its own positioning, before presenting the market-entry case to leadership.
At Sensata, I benchmarked 10+ competitors across EMEA and the US and turned that read into pricing and go-to-market decisions that drove +12% YoY revenue and +8% market share.
I am now running the same discipline at Applied Materials, turning fragmented Sales, Engineering and Accounts input into one market view, with recurring analysis automated through Claude AI agents.
A Strategy Associate who leverages market, competitive and internal data to build hypotheses across travel corridors, issuers and personas is describing this exact loop: size the market, benchmark the field, build the case, repeat it for the next corridor.

First 30 days

What the first month supporting Cross Border strategy and Visa Destinations looks like.

Week 1
Map the existing corridors
Pull the market, competitive and internal data already behind the live destinations (Paris, New York). Sit with the regional and global Cross Border teams to hear which analyses actually moved an investment decision.
Why first: the strongest pattern for a new destination is usually visible in what already worked for an existing one, the same move that opened the Safran engagement.
Week 2-3
Build the persona and sizing model
Turn the strongest patterns into a repeatable hypothesis-and-analysis framework for travel personas (expats, students) and a new destination market, then pressure-test it against a real corridor with the regional team.
Why now: building the model against a real corridor, not in a vacuum, is what made the Safran case defensible in front of leadership.
Week 4
Support the investment case
Package the analysis into an investment-prioritization case for a new destination (Madrid, Tokyo or Egypt), with issuer and market-share implications a global stakeholder can act on quickly.
Why this fast: a strategy analysis only earns its keep once it drives an actual prioritization call.
