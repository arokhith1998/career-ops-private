The sensor-product roadmap I ran at Sensata carried a 100 plus feature backlog across three regions, and the hard part was never writing the roadmap, it was keeping that backlog honest against engineering capacity while a live customer base kept using the product every day. That is the same tension your issuer digital products team is managing at Visa: a full product lifecycle that has to move from concept through launch while an issuer base depends on what is already shipped.

I know that lifecycle from the builder's side too. I shipped SwipeHire alone, an AI job-matching platform on the OpenAI API, writing the PRD, scoping the MVP and running 8+ A/B tests and 30+ customer interviews that lifted activation 25%. At Applied Materials now, Claude AI agents handle the repetitive market analysis so the actual product calls get the attention, the same GenAI-in-the-workflow habit this role is looking for.

What makes issuer digital products a real problem, not just a title, is the two-sided pressure most consumer products do not have: the issuer signs the contract, but the cardholder is who opens the app every day, and a roadmap has to serve both without one crowding out the other. I managed a version of that at Sensata, weighing OEM and distributor input against adoption data across 50K+ connected devices, and building for that kind of pull between two different users is the part of product work I would rather be doing.
