Why this deck exists

A segment RevOps seat for consumption revenue does not lack candidates who understand forecasting. It lacks someone who has already run the full loop, planning through pipeline inspection, on a book several times this size.

What this segment ops seat runs into

Revenue that moves with workload inside existing accounts does not forecast like a flat-contract business; a plan built on new logos alone misses where the number actually comes from.
An MBR or QBR only works if every follow-up has an owner. Without that discipline, the review becomes a status update instead of a decision point.
Most RevOps candidates have built a dashboard. Fewer have been accountable for the margin and pipeline outcome when a forecast call was wrong.

What I have already run, at comparable scale

At Sensata, I built the Bleeders and Leakers program: a $627M pipeline inspected weekly for win rate, stage distribution and margin risk, the same funnel discipline a segment review runs on.
At Applied Materials now, I build the forecasting models that feed quarterly business reviews across multiple product groups, and I pull my own SQL.
At PriceKeel, I run CRM, CPQ, forecast and pipeline reviews, and deal approvals end to end, the systems-and-process ownership this seat is built for.
This req asks for segment planning, operating cadence (MBRs, QBRs, forecast reviews), capacity and quota models, pipeline inspection that surfaces risk early, funnel and consumption analytics, and sales processes built into Salesforce. That is the loop above, run on a business already several times a single segment's size.
