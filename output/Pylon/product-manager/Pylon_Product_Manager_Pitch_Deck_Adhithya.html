Why this deck exists

An agentic support product lives or dies on one judgment call: what the agent handles alone, and what still needs a human read, repeated every week as trust shifts.

What losing that discipline runs into

A product area without that boundary does not fail loudly. It fails as an agent that over-automates and erodes customer trust, or under-automates and ships a dashboard nobody asked for.
Spanning PDE to GTM means Product, Engineering, Sales and CS all read that boundary differently unless one person owns the definition and defends it with evidence.
The fix is not a stricter policy. It is someone who treats discovery, scoping and the automate-versus-verify call as one job, not three separate handoffs.

What I have already run

At Sensata, I owned a 100+ feature backlog end to end with engineering in Agile across 3 regions, the same full-funnel ownership "own an entire product area" is asking for.
At SwipeHire, I wrote the PRD, scoped the MVP and shipped AI-driven matching alone, then used 12+ interviews and 8+ A/B tests to decide what the AI should decide versus what the user still needed to see.
Both mean I have made that automate-versus-verify call myself, not just reviewed someone else's.
A Product Manager who owns a product area end to end, lives the customer persona, and starts with evidence is describing exactly this loop: decide what the agent can be trusted with, defend the call with data, and move the line as trust grows.

First 30 days

What the first month owning a product area at Pylon looks like.

Week 1
Learn the persona and the current automate/verify line
Sit with Support, Engineering and CS to live the target customer persona's workflow, read the tickets and feature requests already on file, and map where the agent currently hands off to a human.
Why first: the same instinct that had me run 12+ interviews before touching SwipeHire's matching logic.
Week 2-3
Own one scoping and launch cycle
Take one piece of the product area from problem definition through a shipped change: scope it with Engineering, test the automate-versus-verify call with real usage data, and bring a point of view to defend in review.
Why now: the same end-to-end habit that shipped SwipeHire's MVP without a team behind it.
Week 4
Close the GTM loop and defend the next iteration
Partner with PMM, sales and customer teams on positioning and enablement for what shipped, then bring the adoption data back to argue for (or against) the next move.
Why this fast: a product area only earns trust if the loop keeps running past the first ship, not just at launch.
