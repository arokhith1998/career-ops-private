Hiring Team, IXL Learning

At Sensata I did not let a 100+ feature backlog tell me what to build next. I ran 30+ interviews with the OEM and distributor engineers who actually used the product, found out which features were theoretical and which were blocking real adoption, and reordered the backlog around that. Time-to-market dropped 20% and CSAT rose 22%, not because the team worked faster, but because we stopped building the wrong things first. That is the habit I would bring to IXL's roadmap: talk to the student, teacher or parent before the PRD, not after.

I would push back gently on the idea that an associate PM's job is mostly writing clear requirements and staying organized. The harder part is getting Engineering and Design to agree on what a requirement actually means before the first sprint starts, and holding that alignment through the inevitable scope fights. I have done that from the product side at Sensata, co-owning a roadmap with Engineering across 3 regions, and from the founder side building SwipeHire, where I wrote the PRD, scoped the MVP, and shipped it to 50+ users myself. Ran 8+ A/B tests and 12+ user interviews on that one, which lifted activation 25%, so I know what it costs to defend a requirement with nobody else to defer to.

IXL's products reach millions of students, teachers and parents, which changes the bar for a feature decision: it is not a guess, it has to hold up across a huge and varied user base. That is the same discipline I ran at Sensata across 50K+ connected devices, finding the cohorts that actually signaled adoption instead of assuming one persona spoke for everyone. I also run Claude AI agents daily at Applied Materials to turn research and competitive signal into decisions faster, so building and defending a roadmap with AI as a working tool, not a buzzword, is already how I operate.

Proof trail at adhi-product.vercel.app/ref/ixl-learning. I would welcome the chance to talk through how this maps to the Associate Product Manager role.
