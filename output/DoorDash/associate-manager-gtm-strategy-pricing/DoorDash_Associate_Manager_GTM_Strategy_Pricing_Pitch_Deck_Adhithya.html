Why this deck exists

A brand-new business line does not get a pricing playbook for free. Someone has to build it while the deals are already closing.

What losing that discipline runs into

A new line without a pricing model does not fail loudly. It fails as a deal priced on gut feel, a margin that slips because nobody root-caused the first bad quarter, and a Sales team improvising terms deal by deal because the playbook does not exist yet.
Business Development, Logistics, Product and Engineering all touch the same new line, so a gap in pricing structure becomes everyone's guessing game the first time a deal gets contested.
The fix is not a spreadsheet after the fact. It is someone who treats pricing, process and margin protection as one build, started on day one.

What I have already run

At Sensata, I led the Bleeders and Leakers program: root-causing margin gaps against competitive data and customer willingness to pay, recovering $700K+ in margin in 2 quarters and raising distributor margins from 53% to 61% while the business kept scaling.
At PriceKeel, I set up and run the CRM, CPQ and deal approvals myself, the same playbook-from-zero discipline a brand-new business line needs on day one.
Both mean I have built the pricing structure and protected the margin on the same business, not handed one off to someone else.
An Associate Manager who strategizes, builds, analyzes and influences across Business Development, Logistics, Product and Engineering to scale a new business line is describing exactly that build, done under speed-to-revenue pressure, not after it.

Turn the first month's pricing data into a lightweight dashboard flagging deals at risk of margin leakage, and hand Sales one guardrail to apply before the next pricing review.
