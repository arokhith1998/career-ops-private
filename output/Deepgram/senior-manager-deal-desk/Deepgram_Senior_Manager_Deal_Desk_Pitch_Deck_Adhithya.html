Why this deck exists

Building a deal desk from zero is not a staffing problem. It is a design problem: get the rules wrong and the desk becomes the bottleneck it was supposed to remove.

What a ground-up desk runs into

Self-serve, enterprise commitments and marketplace deals each need a different approval path, so one playbook has to flex without turning into three sets of exceptions.
Without a pre-approved matrix, every non-standard deal becomes an executive-review meeting, which is the opposite of helping Sales win faster.
Deal margin and realized discount have to be visible by segment before anyone can say which discounts are actually defensible, not just which ones felt reasonable.

What I have already built and run

At PriceKeel, I designed the guardrail engine itself: a rule set that reads a live discount exception against a company's own closed-won and closed-lost deals and recommends an explainable path.
At Sensata, I ran the margin side at scale: $700K+ recovered in 2 quarters and distributor margins lifted 53% to 61%, both decided deal by deal against documented competitive and willingness-to-pay data.
At Applied Materials now, AI agents built on Claude take the first pass on repetitive pricing analysis so my call is the one that moves the deal, the exact loop this desk needs.
This req asks for an approval matrix and playbook most deals never need, same-day answers on the rest, deal-margin and discount-band visibility, audit-ready records in Salesforce, and an AI agent doing the first-pass review. That is the guardrail engine, run at Sensata's scale, done again for Deepgram.
