Why this deck exists

The posting is direct: be the enabler of deal formation and execution without slowing the sales cycle down.

What losing that discipline runs into

A deal desk without a real gatekeeper does not fail loudly. It fails as a non-standard request nobody structured in time, a discount that slips past an early checkpoint, and a revenue-recognition question that surfaces after the deal is already signed.
Navan's own posting names the cross-functional surface area directly: legal, contracts, sales operations, order management, revenue, finance and product management all touch the same deal, so a gap in deal-review judgment becomes everyone's problem.
The fix is not a slower process. It is someone who has already made the pricing and structuring call on live deals, fast enough that sales cycle time does not stretch.

What I have already run, at smaller scale

At Sensata, I led the Bleeders and Leakers program: deal-by-deal negotiation directly against competitive gaps and customer willingness to pay, recovering $700K+ in margin in 2 quarters without stalling deals in review.
At PriceKeel, I built the CRM, CPQ configuration and deal-approval workflow myself, including the guardrail that catches a non-standard deal request before it reaches final review.
Both mean I have sat on the pricing side of a deal review, structuring sophisticated terms rather than only enforcing a checklist.
A Senior Deal Desk Analyst who drives best practices via deal reviews and early checkpoints, while optimizing revenue, profitability and market share, is describing this exact loop: structure the deal, enforce the standard, and keep the cycle moving.
