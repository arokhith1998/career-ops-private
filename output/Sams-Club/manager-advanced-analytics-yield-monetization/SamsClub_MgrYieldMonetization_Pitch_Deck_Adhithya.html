Why this deck exists

Yield and monetization is really one job split three ways: forecast the inventory, allocate it for revenue, and prove the call with performance data, across every vertical and advertiser at once.

What losing that discipline runs into

An advertising portfolio without disciplined allocation does not fail loudly. It fails as a rate card that stops matching demand, a sell-through number nobody can explain, and a new ad format that launches without a revenue case behind it.
Sales, Client Success, Measurement and Finance all touch the same inventory decision, so a gap in forecasting or reporting becomes everyone's guessing game at the next planning cycle, not just one team's problem.
The fix is not another spreadsheet. It is someone who treats forecasting, allocation and reporting as one connected loop instead of three separate jobs.

What I have already run

At Sensata, I built the Power BI system that allocated 1,200 pricing opportunities against a $627M target across 3 regions, 4 product families and 20 product lines, the same forecast-to-allocation loop this role runs across verticals and advertisers.
At Pixis, I carried P&L targets across an 8-client advertising portfolio, reallocating budget and channel mix on Amazon Ads and Criteo as performance data came in.
Both mean I have sat on the inventory-allocation side and the revenue-reporting side of the same decision, not just one or the other.
A Manager who owns inventory forecasting, revenue optimization through sell-through rate and CPMs, and performance-data-driven recommendations is describing this exact loop: forecast it, allocate it, prove it.
