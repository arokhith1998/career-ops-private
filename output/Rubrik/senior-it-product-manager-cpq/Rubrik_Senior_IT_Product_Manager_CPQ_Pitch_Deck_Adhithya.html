Why this deck exists

The req is not "administer a quoting tool." It is moving a rules-based CPQ engine toward AI agents with human-in-the-loop checkpoints, across multi-product and partner channels, without losing Finance's trust in the exception trail.

What that actually requires

A rules engine redesigned so an AI agent can recommend a pricing or discount action, but a human still signs it every time, the only way Product, Sales, Finance, Deal Desk and channel leaders trust the output.
An audit trail that survives a Finance review: who overrode what, against which evidence, not a log nobody opens.
Quote-to-cash that stays simple as product lines, partner tiers and channel structures multiply, not a workaround built per channel.

What I have already run

At PriceKeel, I designed the guardrail engine myself: it recommends a discount guardrail learned from each company's own closed-won and closed-lost deals, and logs every human override.
At Sensata, I repriced parts sold through distribution and raised distributor and channel-partner margins 53% to 61%, keeping quotes consistent across regions and product lines.
At Applied Materials, I run AI agents built on Claude against my own recurring pricing and quoting analysis, the same human-plus-agent workflow this role is scoping.
The through-line: every system here pairs an AI recommendation with a human decision a reviewer can audit later, not an agent making the call alone.

First 30 days

What the first month on Rubrik's CPQ roadmap looks like.

Week 1
Map the rules engine and its stakeholders
Walk the quoting and configuration rules engine end to end with Product, Sales, Finance, Deal Desk and channel leaders, so the first AI-agent checkpoint targets the quote type with the most manual review today, not a guess.
Why first: the same first move behind PriceKeel's guardrail engine, map the exception pattern before automating around it.
Week 2-3
Ship the first human-in-the-loop checkpoint
Stand up one AI-agent recommendation, with a human sign-off and a logged override, on the highest-volume quote or discount type identified in week one.
Why now: the exact guardrail-plus-audit-trail pattern already running at PriceKeel, not a net-new design.
Week 4
Simplify one multi-partner quote path
Pick one multi-product, multi-channel quote flow and cut it down the way the distributor repricing work did at Sensata: fewer manual exceptions, same or better margin.
Why this fast: the same channel-quote discipline that moved Sensata's distributor margins 53% to 61%.
