First 30 days

What the first month inside Innovaccer's CEO's office would look like.

Week 1
Sit inside the real cadence
Shadow the leadership reviews, all-hands and stakeholder meetings already running, and map where OKRs and cross-functional priorities are tracked today, the same audit that grounded the Bleeders and Leakers program before a dashboard existed.
Why first: a CEO's office built on assumptions instead of the real cadence is the fastest way to add noise instead of removing it.
Week 2-3
Ship the first brief
Take one live cross-functional priority, whether a market assessment, a board memo or a blocker between functional leaders, and deliver a decision-ready brief, the same move that turned 1,200 scattered opportunities into one shortlist at Sensata.
Why now: one brief that moves a real decision earns more trust than a week-one list of ideas.
Week 4
Turn the first win into the cadence
Use what the first brief proved to set the recurring rhythm, be it an OKR tracker, an executive-communications calendar or a stakeholder-meeting cadence, that keeps running without being rebuilt from scratch every cycle.
Why this fast: a cadence only earns the CEO's trust once it has already kept one real decision on time.
