Hiring Team, The Indus Valley

In May I founded PriceKeel, a decision-integrity layer for B2B SaaS deal pricing, and became the only person who decides what gets built, which pilot gets run this week, and when a conversation with an investor is worth having. Nobody assigns that work. That is close to the bar you described in your post as the one that actually separates hires: not the interview answer, but what someone does when no one is assessing them.

I have operated that way before PriceKeel existed too. At Sensata I took a program called Bleeders and Leakers from a data problem nobody had prioritized to $700K plus in recovered margin in two quarters, unprompted, and was promoted from Product and Growth Marketing to Pricing Lead in ten months on the strength of it. At Applied Materials I turn scattered input from Sales, Engineering and Accounts into one market-sizing view for the semiconductor group with no standing template to follow, carrying P&L and revenue targets along the way, and I have started automating the repetitive parts of that work with AI agents built on Claude so the analysis keeps pace with a market that moves faster than a quarterly cycle.

Before any of that I hired and managed an 8-person team at Pixis running 8 client accounts, each with its own budget and P&L target, and cut churn 50% by building playbooks off what each account's own data actually said instead of a standard report. A Founder's Office seat asks for the same instinct at company scale: pick up a strategy problem that has no owner yet and carry it until it is finished.
