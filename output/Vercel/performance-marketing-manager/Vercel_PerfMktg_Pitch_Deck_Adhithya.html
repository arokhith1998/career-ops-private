One acquisition motion has to serve a self-serve developer who converts in minutes and an enterprise buyer whose cycle runs months, on the same channels: Google, Meta, LinkedIn, YouTube.
Multi-million-dollar spend only compounds if CAC keeps falling as budget scales, which means the reporting and testing loop has to get faster every quarter, not just the spend.
AI has to be load-bearing in that loop, not a slide: agents and automations that compress campaign turnaround and scale creative production, not just a mention in a tools list.

First 30 days

What the first month owning Vercel's paid acquisition engine looks like.

Week 1
Map spend to the two buyer motions
Pull current spend and CAC by channel and by motion (self-serve signup versus enterprise pipeline), and sit with Product Marketing, Lifecycle and Data to find where the two motions are sharing a campaign that should be split, or splitting one that should share.
Why first: the same first move behind every channel I have owned. The split shows up in the data before anyone has named it as one problem.
Week 2-3
Stand up the AI-assisted testing loop
Build the first agent or automation that removes a manual step from creative or audience testing, whichever is the current bottleneck, and run it against a live test so the win is measured, not theoretical.
Why now: the same move that cut cost per lead 50% at Plug Power and cuts my own reporting time today at Applied Materials.
Week 4
Report the first CAC trendline to leadership
Bring event-based tracking and attribution current enough to show one real trendline: CAC by channel and by motion, moving in the right direction, with the next test already queued behind it.
Why this fast: a clean number beats a plan. Every role on my resume has been measured by the metric moving, not the deck describing it.
