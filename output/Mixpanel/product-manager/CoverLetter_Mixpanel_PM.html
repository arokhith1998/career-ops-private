At Sensata I instrumented KPI frameworks in SQL and GA4 across 50K+ connected industrial devices to find which cohorts were about to churn versus adopt a new feature, before anyone asked me for that report. That is the habit a product analytics company should want baked into its own PM process, not just packaged and sold to customers.

Managing a 100+ feature backlog with engineering, in Agile across 3 regions, only holds up if every line can be defended with a customer signal instead of a hunch. That backlog cut time-to-market 20% and accelerated releases 25%, and the real driver behind those numbers was the 30+ customer interviews that fed the roadmap, not the backlog tool itself.

I also know this from the builder's side of an analytics stack, not just the requester's side. I wrote the PRD and scoped the MVP for SwipeHire, an AI job-matching platform, then wired up GA4 and GTM event tracking myself to run 8+ A/B tests on the activation funnel, lifting activation 25% across the first 50+ users. Instrumenting my own funnel instead of asking someone else to pull the report is the habit I would bring to a PM seat inside the company that builds the instrumentation everyone else runs on.

I would bring that same pattern, ground the roadmap call in the cohort instead of the opinion, to Mixpanel's product team. Proof trail at adhi-product-ai.vercel.app/ref/mixpanel.
