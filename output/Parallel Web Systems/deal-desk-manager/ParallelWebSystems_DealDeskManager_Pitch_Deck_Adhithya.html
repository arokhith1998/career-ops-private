Why this deck exists

The problem is not that Parallel lacks a pricing policy. It is that usage-based AI products throw off a non-standard quote on nearly every enterprise deal, and someone has to decide fast, defensibly, and in a way Legal and Accounting can both book.

What building a deal desk from zero actually requires

Approval matrices and quoting workflows that keep up with AEs selling consumption and committed-spend pricing, not a policy document nobody opens before a live call.
A model of each deal's economics, ramp timing, prepaid commitments, multi-year terms, that an AE can get an answer from in minutes, not after quarter-end pressure has already set in.
A system Legal and Accounting trust enough to let revenue recognition and billing run cleanly, so deal velocity never becomes a billing problem later.

At PriceKeel, I built the discount-guardrail engine and Finance audit trail from zero, the exact approval-matrix-plus-audit-trail this seat stands up for Parallel.
At Sensata, I recovered $700K+ in margin in 2 quarters negotiating non-standard terms deal by deal, and closed AP26 revenue models and multimillion-dollar customer quotes directly with senior executives.

First 30 days

What the first month building Parallel's deal desk from zero looks like.

Week 1
Map every non-standard request from last quarter
Pull every discount exception and custom term AEs requested last quarter, sit with Sales, Legal and Accounting to find exactly where today's process breaks down before writing a single rule.
Why first: the same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers program at Sensata, find the leak before naming the fix.
Week 2-3
Ship the first approval matrix and guardrail
Build the first approval matrix and discount guardrail for the highest-volume deal type, ramp or committed-spend, pressure-test it live with an AE under quarter-end pressure, and ship it as a rule Legal and Accounting can both act on.
Why now: the same deal-by-deal modeling discipline that made the 53% to 61% distributor gain stick instead of drift back.
Week 4
Automate the approval, do not babysit it
Turn the first rule into something the CPQ and quoting system enforces on its own, and route only genuine exceptions to a human call, so deal velocity does not wait on a person reading every quote.
Why this fast: the same AI-agent habit that keeps my own recurring reporting moving at Applied Materials today, not a plan for someday.
