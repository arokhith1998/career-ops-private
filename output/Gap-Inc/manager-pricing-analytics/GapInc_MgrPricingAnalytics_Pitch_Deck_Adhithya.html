Why this deck exists

A pricing-architecture refresh is really three jobs held by one person: discount-budget owner, in-season analyst and pricing-agent validator, across one brand at once.

What losing that discipline runs into

A discount budget without in-season reconciliation does not fail loudly. It fails as a markdown that looked fine in the plan and a margin number that surprises Finance a quarter later.
Merchandising, Planning, Company Planning and Commerce and Experience all touch the same price, so a gap between the guardrail and what actually shipped becomes everyone's guessing game at the next business review.
The fix is not another report. It is someone who treats pricing principles, the discount budget and in-season reconciliation as one connected discipline, the same way this brand is asking its pricing-agent outputs to be checked before they inform a decision.

What I have already run

At Sensata, I owned the strategic discount budget inside the Bleeders and Leakers program and reconciled it in season, recovering $700K+ in margin in 2 quarters and lifting distributor margins from 53% to 61%.
At PriceKeel, I designed the guardrail engine and the audit trail that back every discount exception with evidence Finance can act on, the exact discipline this role measures for adoption and effectiveness.
Both mean I have sat on the discount-decision side and the governance-and-reporting side of the same gate, not just one or the other.
A Manager, Pricing Analytics who owns pricing principles, the discount budget, in-season analytics and pricing-agent validation, while reviewing an analyst's work, is describing this exact loop: set the guardrail, reconcile against it, keep the evidence honest.

First 30 days

What the first month owning pricing analytics for this brand looks like.

Week 1
Learn the brand's pricing architecture and discount guardrails

Week 2-3
Own one in-season reconciliation and scenario cycle

Why now: the same segment-by-segment discipline that made the Bleeders and Leakers dashboard usable in a leadership review.
Week 4
Validate a pricing-agent output and review the analyst's work
Validate one pricing-agent recommendation end to end, document where its logic needs a correction, and give feedback on an in-flight analysis from the analyst on the team.
Why this fast: a pricing guardrail only earns trust if someone checks it every cycle instead of assuming last quarter's logic still holds.
