Why this deck exists

An Entrepreneur in Residence seat asks for two things most product hires never have to prove: start something from nothing, and stay in the build for years, not sprints.

What the seat actually demands

Building a business from zero inside an existing company means there is no backlog to inherit, no revenue model already agreed, and no team until the EIR builds one, whether the mandate lands on Enterprise Growth or New Learning Brand.
The written case for each idea has to hold up in front of the Founders directly, which is a different bar than a normal product update.
Both mandates need the same thing underneath: P&L management, hands-on building for the full 2-3 year run, and a written case strong enough to carry conviction without a safety net.

What I am already running, on exactly that shape of problem

At PriceKeel, I am building a company from zero right now: designed the guardrail engine and audit trail, set up the CRM, CPQ and deal-approval process from scratch, and I am running pilots while raising the company's first round.
At SwipeHire, I wrote the PRD, scoped the MVP and hired the team that shipped an AI job-matching platform to 50+ early users, the same zero-to-one platform mechanics a New Learning Brand needs before its first cohort signs up.
Both required the same discipline: a written plan good enough to move people, a team built around it, and P&L ownership carried the whole way through.
A founder who is currently building a company and who has already shipped one product from a blank page is not two different profiles. It is one operating pattern, and it matches either mandate MedhaviOne is standing up.

04
First 30 days

What the first month as MedhaviOne's Entrepreneur in Residence looks like.

Week 1
Get the mandate and the numbers straight
Sit with the Founders to lock the assigned mandate, whichever one it is, and pull together the market sizing and early customer signal already sitting inside MedhaviOne for it.
Why first: the same first move that shaped PriceKeel's guardrail engine, ground the plan in real numbers before writing a word of strategy.
Week 2-3
Write the plan, build the first version
Turn the mandate into a written plan the Founders can act on, then start building: the first GTM motion for Enterprise Growth, or the first marketplace listings and pricing tiers for New Learning Brand.
Why now: the same sequence that got SwipeHire from a PRD to 50+ users, write it down, then ship the smallest version that tests it.
Week 4
Ship, instrument, and report
Launch the first version to real customers, put an OKR cadence and a reporting rhythm in place, and bring the Founders a written update on what the numbers say to build next.
Why this fast: a mandate is only really owned once it can report its own numbers back, not once someone else has to ask for them.
