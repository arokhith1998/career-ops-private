Why this deck exists

The risk is not slower growth. It is forecasts and quota data that stop agreeing with each other.

What a fast-scaling sales org runs into

Forecasting, pipeline reporting, quota models and territory planning each start in their own spreadsheet or system, and a fix in one place quietly breaks the definition another team is using.
Without one dashboard for funnel health, conversion and revenue performance, capacity and compensation decisions get made on numbers that do not match by the time Finance checks them.
The fix is not another handoff meeting. It is a system that fixes the metric definitions once and a routing habit that keeps CRM and BI data in sync as the team grows.

What I have already run, at smaller scale

At Sensata, I built the Bleeders and Leakers Power BI dashboard: 1,200 pricing opportunities against a $627M target, with one consistent definition of pipeline stage, win rate and margin across 3 regions.
I did not stop at the dashboard. I redesigned the deal structures underneath the worst gaps, which is why the 53% to 61% distributor margin lift held past the first quarter.
I run that same loop today at PriceKeel: CRM and CPQ, forecast and pipeline reviews, and an audit trail Finance can actually point to, because "eliminate the bottleneck" only works if someone owns the system after launch.
Forecasting and pipeline visibility, data governance and consistent metric definitions, and eliminating operational bottlenecks across the sales cycle are describing this exact loop: fix the definition once, build the dashboard on top of it, keep it from drifting as the org scales.

First 30 days

What the first month on Intercom's sales operations team looks like.

Week 1
Map where the data disagrees
Pull forecasting, pipeline and quota reporting as they exist across Sales, Finance and Business Systems today, and trace where two teams are using the same term to mean different things. Sit with GTM leadership and Data Engineering to hear where that friction already shows up.
Why first: the disagreement is usually visible in the data before anyone names it. Same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers program.
Week 2-3
Ship the funnel-health view
Build the dashboard tracking funnel health, conversion and revenue performance against one shared metric dictionary, and pressure-test it with Sales and Finance against the top territory and capacity questions before it goes live.
Why now: ranking against one real target, then verifying with the cross-functional group, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain stick.
Week 4
Standardize the governance
Turn the Week 1 findings into a data governance standard: one set of metric definitions, a change-management path for CRM updates, and the start of a compensation and territory-planning cadence built on the same numbers.
Why this fast: a dashboard matters less if the definitions behind it stay ad hoc. Repeatability is the actual ask behind "data governance."
