The problem this deck solves

Outcomes-based pricing scales a lot of enterprise conversations at once. The hard part is keeping deal-desk rigor identical on the tenth negotiation as on the first.

What breaks as deal volume grows

Every outcomes-based deal needs its own ROI case tied to that customer's actual metrics, so the commercial model cannot be a copy-paste template without losing credibility with Finance and the customer.
Negotiation leverage comes from knowing exactly where the gap is between what a customer currently pays for the problem and what Sierra's outcome is worth, deal by deal, not from a standard discount ladder.
A business case that convinces one Fortune 500 buyer does not automatically convince the next; each one needs its own evidence, built fast enough to not stall the sales cycle.

What I have already run, at comparable scale

At Sensata, I built a $627M commercial model covering 1,200 pricing opportunities across 3 regions and 20 product lines, each one scored on its own competitive gap and customer willingness to pay, not a flat rule.
I sat in the room for the multimillion-dollar customer quotes that model fed, negotiating with senior executives on both sides of the table.
As founder of PriceKeel, I sell a pricing business case myself, every week, into RevOps, Sales leadership and Finance who did not ask for a change.
This req asks for pricing strategy on major enterprise opportunities, commercial models and ROI analyses tied to measurable customer value, and sound judgment in high-stakes negotiations. That is the loop above, already run at scale.
