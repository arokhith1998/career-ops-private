Why this deck exists

The problem is not that Sierra lacks proof of value. It is that every enterprise buyer needs their own investment case.

What a business at this stage runs into

Selling customer-facing AI agents into brands like Rocket Mortgage, SoFi, Sutter Health and SoftBank means every deal has its own operating metrics, its own definition of a win, and its own finance team that has to sign off before the deal closes.
A generic ROI deck does not survive that room. The business case has to be rebuilt, fast, around each customer's own cost structure, revenue lines and loyalty metrics, and it has to hold up when a CFO pushes back.
The fix is not one great case study. It is a repeatable methodology: a framework that turns a new customer's numbers into a rigorous case without starting from a blank page every cycle.

What I have already run, at smaller scale

At Sensata, I ran the Bleeders and Leakers program: a Power BI system that ranked pricing opportunities against a hard revenue target, by region and product line, so leadership knew exactly where the case for action was strongest.
I did not stop at the model. I acted on what it revealed, which is why the 53% to 61% margin gain held past the first quarter instead of drifting back.
I am now building PriceKeel, a business value framework for B2B deal pricing built from the ground up, because the same problem, an unowned methodology for proving value, keeps showing up in every fast-growing company I have worked with.
A business value strategist who develops customer-specific value frameworks, builds ROI models grounded in operating and financial metrics, and establishes that methodology from the ground up is describing this exact loop: rank the opportunity, prove the number, make the proof repeatable for the next enterprise cycle.

First 30 days

What the first month building Sierra's business value framework looks like.

Week 1
Map the existing cases
Pull the ROI stories and investment cases already used in live enterprise cycles. Sit with the pricing team, sales and a handful of recent customers to hear which operating metrics actually moved a CFO, and which ones fell flat.
Why first: the strongest case pattern is usually visible in what already closed. Same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers program.
Week 2-3
Build the repeatable model
Turn the strongest patterns into a rigorous ROI model template grounded in cost, revenue and loyalty metrics, then pressure-test it against 2 to 3 live deals with sales before it goes wide.
Why now: building the model with real deals in the room, not in a vacuum, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain stick.
Week 4
Scale it by industry
Package the template into industry-specific business cases sales can adapt quickly, plus the audit trail that shows finance and operations stakeholders exactly how each number was built.
Why this fast: a framework only earns its name once a second team can run it without you in the room.
