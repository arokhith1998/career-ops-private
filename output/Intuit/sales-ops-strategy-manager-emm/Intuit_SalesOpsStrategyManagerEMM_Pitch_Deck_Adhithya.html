Why this deck exists

The risk in EMM is not a missed target. It is a scorecard three teams stop trusting at the same time.

What a fast-moving mid-market segment runs into

ICPs, segmentation and market sizing age out quickly when a segment is still forming, so a GTM initiative gets measured against targeting data that is already a quarter out of date.
A scorecard that Sales, Finance and leadership each read differently is worse than no scorecard: it lets every team keep its own version of "on track" until the quarter closes.
The fix is not another reporting layer. It is one Finance-reconciled number, an executive narrative built on it, and a habit of re-running the ICP work before it goes stale.

What I have already run, at smaller scale

At Sensata, I built the Bleeders and Leakers scorecard: 1,200 opportunities ranked against a $627M target, one definition of pipeline stage, win rate and margin across 3 regions.
I did not stop at the dashboard. I owned a GTM initiative end to end (new sensing line launch, $2M+ first-year revenue), so I know what a scorecard has to measure before the launch, not just after.
I run that same loop today at PriceKeel: CRM and CPQ, forecast and pipeline reviews, and an audit trail Finance can point to, because a Finance-reconciled model only holds if someone owns it past launch day.
ICPs and segmentation, an EMM scorecard with units/revenue/trend, Finance-reconciled forecasting, and cross-functional adoption of new processes are one loop: fix the targeting, build the number everyone agrees on, then keep it from drifting as the segment moves.

First 30 days

What the first month on Intuit's EMM sales strategy team looks like.

Week 1
Map where the EMM number disagrees
Pull the current EMM scorecard, forecasting model and ICP/segmentation work as they exist across Sales, Finance and GTM teams today, and trace where two teams are using "on track" to mean different things. Sit with GTM and Finance leadership to hear where that friction already shows up.
Why first: the disagreement is usually visible in the data before anyone names it. Same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers program.
Week 2-3
Ship the Finance-reconciled scorecard
Build the EMM scorecard tracking units, revenue pipeline and trend against one shared forecasting framework, and pressure-test it with Sales and Finance against the top targeting and quota questions before it goes live.
Why now: ranking against one real target, then verifying with the cross-functional group, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain stick.
Week 4
Turn it into the executive narrative
Package the Week 1-3 findings into the executive narrative leadership uses to track EMM, plus a change-management path for cross-functional adoption so the new process does not quietly revert after the first review.
Why this fast: a scorecard matters less if the story behind it stays ad hoc. Adoption is the actual ask behind "drive cross-functional adoption."
