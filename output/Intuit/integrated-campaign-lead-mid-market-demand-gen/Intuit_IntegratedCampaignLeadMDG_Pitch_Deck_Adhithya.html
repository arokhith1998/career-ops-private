Why this deck exists

The risk in Mid-Market demand gen is not a missed number. It is a brief three teams stop reading the same way.

What a fast-moving mid-market motion runs into

A campaign brief written for email, paid, web, events and sales ages fast when each channel owner reads "done" differently, so launch readiness slips without anyone naming the slip.
Dependency tracking across a matrixed org is the part that quietly fails first: the brief looks fine, the workback looks fine, and the handoff between two teams is where the real breakdown actually lives.
The fix is not another status meeting. It is one owned campaign architecture, a governance cadence that catches drift early, and the judgment to escalate before a launch date, not after.

What I have already run, at smaller scale

At Sensata, I owned a GTM launch end to end across North America and Europe: positioning, pricing, channel plan and cross-channel execution across email and paid media, closing $2M+ in first-year revenue.
At Plug Power, I ran $100K/month across Paid Search, Paid Social, SEO and lifecycle email as one integrated plan, then unified the Mar-Tech stack so dependency tracking lived in one place.
I run the escalation side of this today too: at Pixis, I cut client churn 50% by making the call before an account went sideways, the same judgment a matrixed campaign org needs.
Campaign architecture, cross-channel orchestration, launch readiness and connecting performance to business outcomes are one loop: own the brief, run every channel against it, govern the cadence, then prove it moved pipeline.

First 30 days

What the first month on Intuit's Mid-Market demand-gen team looks like.

Week 1
Map where the current brief and the handoff disagree
Pull the live Mid-Market campaign briefs, workbacks and readiness docs across email, paid, web, events and sales, and trace where two channel owners read "ready to launch" differently. Sit with the matrixed stakeholder group to hear where that friction already surfaces.
Why first: the disagreement is visible in the workback before anyone names it. Same move that started the Plug Power Mar-Tech unification.
Week 2-3
Ship one campaign architecture and dependency tracker
Build the integrated campaign brief and dependency tracker that email, paid, web, events and sales all read from, and pressure-test launch readiness against it with Sales before the first Mid-Market motion goes live.
Why now: one shared tracker, verified with the cross-functional group early, is what kept the Plug Power channels from drifting apart.
Week 4
Turn it into the operating cadence
Package the Week 1-3 findings into the execution governance cadence leadership uses to track Mid-Market campaigns, plus the escalation path that flags a slipping dependency before launch day instead of after.
Why this fast: a tracker matters less if the cadence behind it stays ad hoc. Governance is the actual ask behind "execution governance and operating cadences."
