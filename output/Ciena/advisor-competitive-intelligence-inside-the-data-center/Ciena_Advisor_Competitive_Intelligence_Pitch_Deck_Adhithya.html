Why this deck exists

Competitive intelligence that sits in a slide deck is a history lesson. Competitive intelligence that reaches Sales, PLM and the CTO office before the next launch is a weapon.

What a fast-moving, technical hardware market does to a CI function

Signal on a rival's next move in AI cluster architectures, coherent optics or pluggable optics arrives in pieces, from Sales, Marketing, PLM, the CTO office and R&D, each with their own angle and urgency.
Without one person owning the synthesis, battlecards go stale, messaging drifts between teams, and the briefing reaches an executive after the decision window, not before it.
The fix is not another one-off competitor slide. It is one analyst who owns the synthesis, keeps the briefing current, and ties every finding back to a decision someone is about to make.

What I have already run, hands on

At Sensata, I benchmarked 10+ competitors across the Industrial BU and turned the findings into a repricing call that drove +12% YoY revenue and +8% market share, not a report that sat unread.
At Applied Materials, I size a fast-moving technical market with Sales, Engineering and Accounts, the same cross-functional input loop this role runs with Sales, Marketing, PLM, CTO and R&D.
At Safran, I led a 5-consultant team that benchmarked 10+ competitors across 150+ facilities and briefed the result straight to leadership.
Analyze competitors' strategy and moves, align messaging across Sales, Marketing, PLM, CTO and R&D, and produce briefings that get acted on: this is the exact loop I already run, just on a different hardware category.
