Why this deck exists

The role is not "write the messaging." It is turning technical product depth into a narrative three different teams can sell the same way.

What that actually requires

An enterprise buyer does not evaluate a feature list. They need product capability translated into a buyer-focused narrative that survives a procurement review, not just a demo.
Product, Sales and Marketing each package an offering differently unless someone owns the translation between them, and that ownership has to hold across presentations, case studies and white papers at once.
AI has to be load-bearing in how that content ships, not a line in a tools list, because an ambiguous, fast-moving environment does not wait for a slow content cycle.

What I have already run

At Sensata, I turned new sensing products into a GTM and positioning story that non-technical buyers could act on, driving $2M+ in first-year revenue.
At Sensata again, I built the sales enablement, presentations and case studies that carried 50+ reps through live enterprise deals, and win rates moved 30%.
At Applied Materials today, I automate my own recurring market-sizing and forecasting work with AI agents built on Claude, the exact fluency this posting names by name.
The through-line: every narrative I have shipped had to survive contact with Sales, Engineering and a skeptical enterprise buyer at the same time, and I have already made AI part of how that content gets produced, not a slide about AI.

First 30 days

What the first month owning Pendo's enterprise narrative looks like.

Week 1
Pressure-test the current narrative
Sit with Product, Sales and the CS team that lives inside product usage data every day, and map where the current enterprise story does and does not survive a procurement conversation. Pull the last 10-15 lost enterprise deals for pattern signal.
Why first: the same first move behind the Sensata GTM work, listening to where the message breaks before rewriting it.
Week 2-3
Rebuild one packaging story with Product and Sales
Pick the offering where technical capability and buyer-facing narrative sit furthest apart, and rebuild its positioning, presentations and case study together with the teams that will actually sell it, not for them.
Why now: the same cross-functional build that moved Sensata's win rate 30% instead of shipping a deck no one used.
Week 4
Stand up the AI-assisted content loop
Build the first agent or automation that removes a manual step from turning competitive research or customer interviews into enterprise-facing content, and run it against a live piece of collateral so the win is measured.
Why this fast: the same habit that keeps my own reporting moving at Applied Materials today, not a plan for someday.
