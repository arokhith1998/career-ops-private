Why this deck exists

A BU pricing analyst role does not lack candidates who know pricing theory. It lacks someone who has already protected margin against real cost and competitive swings, twice, inside semiconductor-industry companies.

What a BU pricing seat runs into

ASP and demand trends move faster than a quarterly pricing review, so a cost change needs to turn into a recommended price action before margin erodes, not after.
The recommendation has to survive contact with Sales, Finance and Operations at once, in a business case and an executive dashboard, not just a spreadsheet.
Most pricing candidates have analyzed a market. Fewer have owned the BU-level P&L outcome when the price action was wrong.

What I have already run, at comparable scale

At Sensata, I led global pricing for the Industrial BU and recovered $700K+ in margin in 2 quarters, closing gaps a competitive and willingness-to-pay review surfaced.
At Applied Materials now, I build competitive, value-based pricing and inflection-point forecasts for a semiconductor equipment business, carrying P&L and revenue targets.
At PriceKeel, I built the guardrail logic that explains why a discount is or is not safe, the same judgment a cost-driven pricing action needs.
This req asks for ASP and competitive-positioning analysis, cost-driven pricing actions, business cases, dashboards, and cross-functional alignment across the BU, Pricing, Finance, Sales and Operations. That is the loop above, run on two different semiconductor-adjacent books of business.

First 30 days

What the first month on onsemi's AMG and ISG pricing desk looks like.

Week 1
Map ASP and margin by product line
Sit with Pricing, Finance and Sales to build one view of ASP trends and margin by BU, product line and region, the same first move that built the Bleeders and Leakers dashboard at Sensata.
Why first: a pricing action is only as good as the cost-and-margin view it is built on.
Week 2-3
Flag the first margin-risk cases
Pressure-test 2 to 3 live cost-change or competitive-positioning signals with Sales and Operations, and draft the business case for the first recommended pricing action.
Why now: building the recommendation against a real signal, not in a vacuum, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain hold at Sensata.
Week 4
Ship the first executive dashboard
Automate one recurring piece of the ASP or margin-tracking workflow, and present the first executive summary that aligns the BU, Pricing, Finance and Sales on the recommendation.
Why this fast: the same habit that keeps Claude agents running recurring pricing analysis at Applied Materials today.
