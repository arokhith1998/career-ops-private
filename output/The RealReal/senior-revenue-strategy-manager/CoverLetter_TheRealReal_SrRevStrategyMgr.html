At Sensata I built the business case and governance structure for the Bleeders and Leakers program, a cross-functional revenue initiative that ran through Sales, Finance and Product. The Power BI dashboard tracked 1,200 opportunities against a $627M target across 3 regions and 20 product lines, surfaced $433M in margin gaps, and recovered $700K plus in margin in two quarters because leadership could see exactly where to push. That is the same loop your posting describes: identify the opportunity, build the business case, define the KPIs up front, and run the governance that keeps a cross-functional team moving.

I like that this role sits at the intersection of Sales, Marketing, Product, Merchandising, Finance and Operations instead of reporting into one of them. Running PriceKeel taught me the same lesson from the inside: I built its CRM, CPQ, forecast and pipeline review cadence and the deal-approval workflow Finance checks before anything closes, because a shared process beats a plan that only lives in one function's head. At Applied Materials, I carry P&L and revenue targets and run the OKRs and quarterly business reviews that turn Sales, Engineering and Account input into one forecast leadership can act on, automating the repetitive parts of that analysis with Claude AI agents.

Your posting is honest that this is a program manager and orchestrator role first, not a strategy-slides role. I would treat the project charter the same way I treated the Bleeders and Leakers rollout: milestones that are specific enough to flag a bottleneck before it becomes a delay, and a post-launch review that actually feeds the next initiative instead of getting filed away. The 40 million members and the authentication-heavy supply chain behind them mean retention and channel-optimization initiatives have to hold up operationally, not just on a slide, which is exactly the kind of execution-tested business case I would want to bring in the door.
