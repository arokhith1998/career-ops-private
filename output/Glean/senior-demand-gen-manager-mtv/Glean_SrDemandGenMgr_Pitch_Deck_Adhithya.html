Why this deck exists

The role is not "run more campaigns." It is one system: a wide net for net-new leads and a tight ABM motion for high-value accounts, feeding the same BDR queue.

What that actually requires

A content syndication, nurture and webinar calendar that keeps producing net-new leads without the cost per lead creeping up as volume scales.
An ABM track for the accounts big enough to earn a direct-mail or demo-program touch, built so BDRs trust where the lead came from instead of treating it as a cold handoff.
A reporting layer that closes the loop from channel to opportunity, so budget and timeline decisions are made on real conversion data, not volume alone.

What I have already run

At Plug Power, I automated the lead-flow pipeline end to end, cutting cost per lead 50% while growing the database, and closed the loop on Salesforce-connected reporting across the full stack.
At Pixis, I ran an account-based retention strategy across an 8-client portfolio, the same segmented, account-level discipline an ABM program for high-value accounts runs on.
At Sensata, I built the sales enablement and BDR-facing follow-through that lifted win rates 30%, so a campaign calendar does not stall at the handoff.
The through-line: every channel I have owned fed a number sales was accountable for, not a lead count that stopped at marketing's dashboard.

First 30 days

What the first month owning demand generation and ABM at Glean looks like.

Week 1
Map the channel-to-BDR handoff
Trace the last 5-10 campaigns across content syndication, nurtures and webinars to find where lead quality drops before the BDR follow-up, and where the ABM track and the broad-net motion are competing for the same accounts instead of splitting the work.
Why first: the same first move behind the Plug Power automation, find where the flow breaks before rebuilding it.
Week 2-3
Stand up one webinar and one ABM pilot
Ship a webinar end to end, concept through promotion and follow-up, alongside a direct-mail or demo-program pilot for a short list of high-value accounts, built with BDRs and sales from day one so the handoff is agreed before either program launches.
Why now: the same cross-functional build that moved Sensata's win rate 30% instead of a program sales never adopted.
Week 4
Close the loop on reporting
Stand up the conversion and cost-effectiveness read behind the Pixis ROAS work for every active channel, then automate the recurring reporting step with an AI agent so budget and timeline calls are made on this cycle's data, not last quarter's.
Why this fast: the same habit that keeps my own reporting moving at Applied Materials today, not a plan for someday.
