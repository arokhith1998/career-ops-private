Why this deck exists

A Senior Pricing Analyst sits between the data and the decision, across price bands, plan types and channels all at once.

What losing that discipline runs into

A pricing program without segmented performance tracking does not fail loudly. It fails as a price change nobody can trace back to a hypothesis, a dashboard that cannot say why a channel's performance slipped, and a test result nobody can tell apart from seasonality.
Product, Finance, Actuarial and Data Science all touch the same pricing decision, so a gap in segmentation or documentation becomes everyone's guessing game at the next review, not just one team's problem.
The fix is not another spreadsheet. It is someone who treats segmentation, modeling and documentation as one connected discipline instead of three separate jobs.

What I have already run

At Sensata, I segmented 1,200 pricing opportunities by region, product family and product line against a $627M target, the same price-band and program-level analysis this role runs across device and protection plans.
At PriceKeel, I built the audit trail and the comparison-group guardrail logic a pricing decision gets checked against before it goes live, the traceable documentation this role keeps for test designs and approvals.
Both mean I have sat on the segmentation side and the documentation side of the same pricing decision, not just one or the other.
A Senior Pricing Analyst who builds statistical models, designs structured pricing tests and documents every assumption is describing this exact loop: segment the data, model the opportunity, keep the decision traceable.

The req, mapped

What the Senior Pricing Analyst opening asks for, and where the proof already sits.

What the role covers

Analyze performance across products, price bands, plan types, clients, markets, channels, and customer groups, including relationships among price, sales volume, attachment or conversion, revenue, margin, and loss costs.
Build, validate, document, and maintain statistical models using methods such as regression, forecasting, segmentation, time-series analysis, and other appropriate quantitative techniques.
Design and evaluate structured pricing tests with defined hypotheses, comparison groups, success measures, sample requirements, decision criteria, and business guardrails.
Maintain traceable documentation for data sources, model versions, analytical assumptions, test designs, approvals, pricing changes, and outcomes.

My Proof

Price-band and segment analysis: segmented 1,200 pricing opportunities by region, product family and product line against a $627M target at Sensata, recovering $700K+ in margin in 2 quarters.
Statistical modeling: built R-based regression models to forecast demand elasticity and profit-maximizing price points, and build inflection-point forecasting models at Applied Materials.
Guardrails and comparison logic: built the guardrail engine at PriceKeel that recommends a discount boundary learned from a company's own closed-won and closed-lost deal comparison groups.
Traceable documentation: built the audit trail that gives Finance an explainable record behind every pricing decision at PriceKeel, and documented the repriced discount logic behind Sensata's distributor-margin lift for governance review.
