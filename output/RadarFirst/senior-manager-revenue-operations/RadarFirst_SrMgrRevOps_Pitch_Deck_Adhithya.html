Why this deck exists

Deal desk negotiation, CPQ governance and AI automation are one connected loop, not three separate hires.

What losing that discipline runs into

A deal desk that negotiates terms but does not own the CPQ workflow behind them ends up re-litigating the same nonstandard-term exception every quarter, because the approval routing lives in someone else's head.
Forecasting, pipeline reporting and sales compensation all draw on the same revenue data, so a gap between the deal desk and the dashboard becomes an attainment dispute a quarter later instead of a decision made on time.
An AI agent that eliminates manual work only stays dependable if the person who built it also owns the access controls and the human-review step. Otherwise it is a demo, not infrastructure.

What I have already run

At PriceKeel, I run the company's own CRM, CPQ configuration and deal-approval workflow myself, resolving nonstandard-term exceptions and giving Finance an auditable trail for every pricing call, the same loop a Deal Desk function needs end to end.
At Sensata, I built the $627M Bleeders and Leakers forecasting dashboard and used it to negotiate multimillion-dollar customer quotes and AP26 revenue scenarios with senior executives, closing the gap between the deal and the number.
At Applied Materials, I automate repetitive revenue analysis with AI agents built on Claude, the same platform this role names for research, enrichment and reporting automation.
A Senior Manager of Revenue Operations who moves comfortably from negotiating an enterprise deal to improving Salesforce workflows to building an AI agent that eliminates manual work is describing exactly this loop: negotiate the deal, govern the system behind it, automate the part that should not need a human every time.
