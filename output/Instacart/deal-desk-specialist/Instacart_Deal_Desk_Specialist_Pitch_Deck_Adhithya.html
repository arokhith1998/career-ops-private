Why this deck exists

Your posting names five things deals need at once: velocity, risk review, a clean Quote-to-Cash process, cross-functional trust and scalable new programs.

What losing that discipline runs into

A deal desk without fast, correct review does not fail loudly. It fails as a quote stuck in approval while Sales waits, a contract term nobody flagged until after signature, and a Quote-to-Cash process that works until a new pricing model or acquisition hits it.
Sales, Finance, Tax and Legal all touch the same deal, so a gap in any one review step becomes everyone's cleanup problem at close, not just Deal Desk's.
The fix is not another approval queue. It is someone who treats deal review, system ownership and governance as one connected loop instead of three handoffs.

What I have already run

At PriceKeel, I set up and run the CRM, CPQ configuration, forecast and pipeline reviews, and deal approvals myself, the same Quote-to-Cash ownership this role holds for Instacart's Sales org.
At Sensata, I read multimillion-dollar customer quotes and AP26 revenue models for risk and approved terms before signature, then scaled that discipline into a program covering 1,200 pricing opportunities.
Both mean I have sat on the deal-review side and the system-ownership side of the same approval, not just one or the other.
A Deal Desk Specialist who owns deal velocity, risk and compliance, Quote-to-Cash excellence, cross-functional partnership and scalable new programs is describing this exact loop: review the deal, run the system, keep the trail honest.

First 30 days

What the first month owning deal desk at Instacart looks like.

Week 1
Learn the Quote-to-Cash flow and approval policies
Sit with Sales, Finance, Tax and Legal to walk the current SFDC/CPQ setup, discount-approval policies and ASC 606 checkpoints, and shadow one deal end to end from quote to booked revenue.
Why first: the same instinct that had me learn PriceKeel's own CRM and CPQ configuration before building anything on top of it.
Week 2-3
Own a deal-review and risk-flagging cycle
Take ownership of a live batch of deals end to end: review pricing and discount structure for approval, read each contract for risk, and flag anything that needs Legal or Tax before it reaches signature.
Why now: the same contract-risk discipline that let me approve multimillion-dollar customer quotes at Sensata without surprises after signature.
Week 4
Document the playbook for a new program
Pick one scalable new program, a pricing model or product line, and document the approval playbook and CPQ setup needed before volume arrives, then train the Sales team on when to engage Deal Desk.
Why this fast: a shared playbook only earns its keep if it is written down before the volume hits, not after.
