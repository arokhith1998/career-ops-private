Hiring Team, Gushwork

At Pixis I ran an 8-person team across 8 client accounts, each with its own media budget and P&L target, and cut churn 50% by swapping one generic report for vertical playbooks built off what each account's own data was actually saying. That is the same operating problem I read into this Chief of Staff seat: Gushwork runs SEO and content programs for a coalition of clients across very different verticals, priced and delivered mostly through AI agents, and someone has to keep that whole portfolio pointed at the business outcome the client feels, not the metric that is easiest to report.

I already trust an agent to do part of my own job. At Applied Materials I built AI agents on Claude to automate the repetitive research and reporting behind my market-sizing and pricing work, on top of running the OKR cadence and leadership decks behind weekly and quarterly business reviews and carrying P&L targets. At Sensata I sat close enough to the executive team to build the AP26 revenue models and site-transition strategy directly with them, and recovered $700K plus in margin in two quarters by taking a pricing program from a data problem to a negotiated outcome. Both jobs required turning other people's fragmented context into one decision leadership could act on, which is the actual job description of a Chief of Staff no matter what the title says.

I am also building PriceKeel right now, a decision-integrity layer for B2B SaaS deal pricing, as the sole operator running pilots, the CRM and pipeline reviews, and the fundraising conversations myself. An AI-native services company that reports on traffic and revenue instead of vanity clicks needs a Chief of Staff who has already sat in that solo-operator seat, not someone learning the instinct for the first time on the job.
