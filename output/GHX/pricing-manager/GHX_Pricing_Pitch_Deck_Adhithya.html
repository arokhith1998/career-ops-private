Why this deck exists

The problem is not that GHX lacks pricing data. It is that SKUs, pricing rules and discount approvals live in three different places, so no one owns the whole commercialization loop.

What owning commercialization at a multi-product platform runs into

A SKU library that grows faster than its governance: every new offering needs requirements, pricing rules and approval workflows before anyone can sell it, and a missed rule shows up as an in-flight contract exception months later.
CPQ configuration, discount approvals and pricing strategy sit in different teams' heads, Product, Sales, Deal Desk, Contract Ops and FP&A, so a process only scales if all of them can work off the same source of truth.
Catalog rationalization is a one-time audit until someone builds the governance that keeps it rationalized: naming conventions, intake workflows and hygiene reviews that outlive the audit itself.

First 30 days

What the first month owning GHX's commercialization process looks like.

Week 1
Map the SKU and discount surface
Pull the last quarter's SKU creation requests, discount exceptions and pricing changes by product line. Sit with Product Management, Deal Desk, Contract Ops and FP&A to hear where the current CPQ setup breaks down on a real deal.
Why first: the leak is usually visible in the data before anyone has named it out loud. Same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers program.
Week 2-3
Audit and pressure-test
Run a structured SKU audit against historical sales and discounting data, rationalize the top offenders, then document the governance, naming conventions, intake workflow and approval rules, that keeps the catalog clean after the audit ends.
Why now: modeling against a hard target, then verifying the fix cross-functionally, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain stick instead of drifting back.
Week 4
Ship the playbook, start the automation roadmap
Turn the audit into the commercialization playbook's first version, the single source of truth for pricing, SKUs and business rules, and flag the first AI/automation opportunity in CPQ worth prioritizing.
Why this fast: a playbook only earns adoption once it saves someone a real decision, the same instinct behind PriceKeel's audit trail.
