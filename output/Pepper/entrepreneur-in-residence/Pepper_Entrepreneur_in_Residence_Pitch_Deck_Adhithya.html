Why this deck exists

An Entrepreneur in Residence at Pepper gets handed two different problems: the one that does not exist yet, and the one that exists but has no owner.

What the seat actually demands

A business line that needs to be rebuilt from zero has no backlog to inherit and no revenue model until someone writes one. That is a founder problem.
A function that is founder-led, ad-hoc or held together by heroics is the harder twin: the team and the process already half-exist, and the job is to step in, stabilize it, and hand it off clean.
Both need the same person to take a sentence instead of a brief, write the playbook, run it, prove it, then make it someone else's job.

What I have already run, on both sides of that problem

At PriceKeel, I am building a product from inception right now: designed the guardrail engine and audit trail, set up the CRM, CPQ and deal-approval process from zero, and am running pilots while raising the first round.
At Sensata, I took a margin line with no real owner and built the Bleeders and Leakers program from scratch, then handed the reporting cadence to the team once it ran on its own.
Both took the same discipline: a number nobody had mapped, a team pointed at the highest-leverage fix, and a handoff that stuck.
A founder who builds from zero and an operator who fixes an unowned function are not two different profiles. It is one operating pattern, run on both a founding problem and a fixing problem, which is exactly the range this seat asks for.

First 30 days

What the first month as Pepper's Entrepreneur in Residence looks like.

Week 1
Map what is unowned
Sit with the Founder and the COE team to find which business lines, functions and manual workflows are ad-hoc or held together by heroics, and get into live US enterprise accounts to hear retention and churn firsthand.
Why first: the strongest opportunity is usually visible in what already exists. Same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers program at Sensata.
Week 2-3
Build the operating layer
Turn the highest-leverage workflow into a definition, a dashboard and a review cadence the Founder can see within 24 hours, and partner with product and engineering to rebuild the manual steps AI-first.
Why now: building the model with the real stakeholders in the room, not in a vacuum, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain stick.
Week 4
Ship v1 and build the bench
Launch a working v1 rather than wait for a perfect plan, measure it against margin, CAC, retention or payback, then start hiring and coaching whoever takes it over next.
Why this fast: a function only counts as fixed once it can run without you in the room, and you have already moved to the next problem.
