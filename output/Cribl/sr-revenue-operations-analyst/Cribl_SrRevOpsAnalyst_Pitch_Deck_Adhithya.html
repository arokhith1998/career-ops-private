Why this deck exists

The risk in a fast-growing, remote-first GTM org is not that segmentation models exist. It is that the account and territory data under them drifts as the team scales.

What a scaling multi-timezone sales org runs into

Segmentation, territory design and compensation plans get modeled once for annual planning, then nobody keeps the underlying account, opportunity and territory data synced to them as reps, deals and quotas change week to week.
Salesforce, Gong and Omni dashboards quietly drift apart from each other: a field goes stale on one side and nobody notices until a VP questions a number in a business review.
The fix is not another dashboard. It is one person who owns the data-quality work before the strategic model gets built on top of it, and who can defend the number when a VP asks.

What I have already run, hands on

At Sensata, I built the Power BI layer that segmented 1,200 opportunities by region, product line and salesperson, so a $627M target stopped being one blended number and became a diagnosable pipeline.
At PriceKeel, I run CRM and CPQ myself and documented the audit trail Finance and Sales check every discount decision against, the same discipline a Salesforce workflow needs to stay trustworthy at scale.
At Plug Power, I automated the Mar-Tech stack across Salesforce, Adobe Analytics, GA and Google Tag Manager so closed-loop reporting ran without a manual reconciliation every week.
Segmentation models, territory design and compensation plans are only as good as the account, opportunity and territory data under them. I build that data integrity first, then the model a Sales VP can actually trust in a business review.

First 30 days

What the first month on Cribl's Revenue Operations team looks like.

Week 1
Trace today's segmentation and data integrity
Map how account, opportunity and territory data flows into the Salesforce, Gong and Omni dashboards today, and flag where a field or definition has drifted between systems before it shows up as a wrong number in a business review.
Why first: the drift is usually visible in the raw data before anyone names it, the same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers segmentation at Sensata.
Week 2-3
Fix the highest-drift workflow
Rebuild the CRM workflow or data-quality rule causing the worst drift, document the change, and pressure-test it with the Sales VP and Finance it feeds before it goes live in a business review.
Why now: the same sequence, segment then fix then document, recovered $700K+ in margin at Sensata and held up past the first quarter.
Week 4
Run the first pipeline diagnostic
Bring the cleaned segmentation into the next business review as a pipeline diagnostic a Sales VP can act on directly, and set the cadence for keeping territory and account data in sync going forward.
Why this fast: a data fix nobody sees in a real business review does not stick. Putting it in front of a VP in week 4 is what makes it permanent.
