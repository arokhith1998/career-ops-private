Why this deck exists

The posting is not "write the positioning." It is making an enterprise buyer trust a conversational-agent platform enough to replace a process they already understand: the hold queue.

Positioning has to survive a technical, enterprise reviewer who already knows what a support queue costs them, not a generic feature pitch.
Launches, thought leadership and sales enablement all have to ship fast and stay consistent, inside a company that runs high-velocity, lean, autonomous teams.
Industry-focused landing pages mean the same platform has to be framed differently for a bank's support queue than for a retailer's.

First 30 days

What the first month owning positioning for ElevenAgents' enterprise business looks like.

Week 1
Sit inside the enterprise buyer conversation
Pull the last 10-15 enterprise deals where a buyer evaluated ElevenAgents against a support queue they already run, and map where today's positioning does and does not survive a technical reviewer's questions.
Why first: the same first move behind the Sensata GTM work, listening to where the message breaks before rewriting it.
Week 2-3
Rebuild one industry landing page and its enablement
Pick the vertical where packaging and sales content sit furthest apart, and rebuild its industry-focused landing page and sales enablement together with the team that owns ElevenAgents.
Why now: the same cross-functional build that moved Sensata's win rate 30% instead of shipping a deck no one used.
Week 4
Stand up the AI-assisted content loop
Build the first agent or automation that removes a manual step from turning customer interviews or competitive research into thought-leadership or enablement content, and run it against a live asset so the win is measured.
Why this fast: the same habit that keeps my own reporting moving at Applied Materials today, not a plan for someday.
