Your post asks for a single source of truth for GTM performance, something that shows what is working by team, channel and rep, not just at the aggregate level. I built exactly that at Sensata: a Power BI dashboard that scored 1,200 pricing opportunities against a $627M target across 3 regions, 4 product families and 20 product lines, with one win-rate and stage definition that Sales and Finance both signed off on, broken out by salesperson and product line. It became the number both teams showed up to the room agreeing on instead of arguing over, which is the exact gap a GTM source of truth needs to close.

I used that same dashboard to go from reporting to recommendation: it surfaced $433M in margin gaps, and I turned the worst of them into $700K+ recovered in two quarters by repricing fee structures where the data showed clear willingness to pay. I also redesigned packaging on distributor contracts, raising channel-partner margins from 53% to 61%, close to the plan-and-fee-structure work your posting calls out under pricing and monetization. None of it was a one-off exercise. I built the reporting cadence, stage definitions and regional scorecards that kept leadership and the ops team aligned as targets shifted quarter to quarter.

I run the same discipline today as founder of PriceKeel: I set up the company's own CRM and CPQ, forecast and pipeline reviews, and deal approvals, and built the audit trail Finance can point to for every pricing call, the source-of-truth habit a GTM analytics function lives or dies by. At Applied Materials I build forecasting models across multiple product groups and carry P&L and revenue targets, and I have started automating the recurring parts of that reporting with Claude AI agents.
