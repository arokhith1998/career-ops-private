Why this deck exists

A small but mighty Deal Desk function is really three jobs held well by one person, quote support, system administration and governance.

What losing that discipline runs into

A quote queue without deal-by-deal review does not fail loudly. It fails as an order form approved on last quarter's pricing logic, a CPQ price book nobody updated, and a month-end crunch where nobody can say which deal is actually blocked.
Sales, Finance, Legal and Product all touch the same quote, so a gap in CPQ or CLM data hygiene becomes everyone's guessing game at the next approval, not just one team's problem.
The fix is not another spreadsheet. It is someone who treats quote creation, system configuration and the governance trail as one connected discipline instead of three separate jobs.

What I have already run

At PriceKeel, I set up and run the CRM, CPQ configuration and the audit trail myself, the same system-administration and governance loop this role holds for Roadrunner and Ironclad.
At Sensata, I owned deal-by-deal quotation review and non-standard contract negotiation inside the Bleeders and Leakers program, then built the Power BI dashboard that kept 1,200 pricing opportunities auditable by segment.
Both mean I have sat on the quote-approval side and the system-configuration side of the same gate, not just one or the other.
A Senior Deal Desk Analyst who delivers quote-to-signature support, administers CPQ and CLM, and keeps Salesforce data clean is describing this exact loop: review the deal, configure the system, keep the governance trail honest.
