Why this deck exists

The problem is not that Quest lacks pricing data. It is that margin leakage hides across two portfolios sold together.

What running two portfolios at once runs into

Cybersecurity and platform management get sold side by side, on the same accounts, often on the same quote, so a discount granted on one line has to make sense next to the price on the other.
Every exception approved without one coherent pricing strategy, not two disconnected ones, becomes a small margin leak, and small leaks compound fast across a multi-product portfolio.
The fix is not more competitive analysis in a vacuum. It is financial modeling and cross-functional collaboration that turn that analysis into a pricing decision Sales and Finance both hold to.

What I have already run, at smaller scale

At Sensata, I led global pricing strategy for a multi-$100M Industrial BU across multiple product lines at once, the same "more than one portfolio, one pricing logic" problem.
I ran the Bleeders and Leakers program: a Power BI system that ranked pricing opportunities by region and product family against a hard revenue target, so leadership knew exactly where to act.
I am now building PriceKeel, a tool that gives Finance an audit trail for every discount decision, because the leak comes back if nobody can explain why the last exception across a portfolio was approved.
A Strategic Pricing Lead who develops pricing strategy, runs competitive analysis and financial modeling, and collaborates cross-functionally across cybersecurity and platform management, is describing this exact loop: one pricing logic for two portfolios, proven with a model, and held together across teams.

First 30 days

What the first month on Quest's pricing desk looks like.

Week 1
Map both portfolios
Pull the last 90 days of discount exceptions and quotes spanning cybersecurity and platform management. Sit with Sales, Finance and Product to hear where a single-portfolio pricing rule breaks down on a two-portfolio deal.
Why first: the leak is usually visible in the data before anyone has named it out loud. Same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers program.
Week 2-3
Model and pressure-test
Build a financial model ranking the top pricing-strategy opportunities against a real target, then pressure-test the top 3 with Sales and Finance before proposing a fix. Ship one pricing recommendation with a documented, cross-functionally-agreed reason.
Why now: modeling against a hard target, then verifying the fix cross-functionally, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain stick.
Week 4
Make it repeatable
Turn the one-off fix into a template: a competitive-analysis view, a financial-modeling format and a checklist Sales and Finance both sign off on before the next cross-portfolio pricing question comes in.
Why this fast: pricing strategy matters less if it only works once. Repeatability is the actual ask behind "cross-functional collaboration."
