Why this deck exists

A market strategy seat that also chases new business cannot stop at analysis. It has to hold up across a negotiation.

What this combination runs into

Opportunity assessments, competitor pipelines and financial files are the easy half. The harder half is carrying that same number into a discovery conversation, a proposal, or a contract term and having it still hold up when the other side pushes back.
A market-sizing model that never meets a real deal stays theoretical. A proposal built without the underlying financial and competitive analysis gets picked apart in the room.
The fix is one person who builds the number and is comfortable sitting across the table defending it, not two separate functions that hand off at the proposal stage.

What I have already run

At Sensata, I built the Bleeders and Leakers dashboard from raw sales and pricing files, then personally closed $700K+ in 2 quarters through negotiations grounded in that same competitive and financial analysis.
I repriced distributor contract terms, lifting margins 53% to 61%, and shaped multimillion-dollar customer contracts and AP26 revenue models directly alongside senior executives.
At Applied Materials now, I size a fast-moving market and feed that view into pricing and the product roadmap, the same input a new-business pitch needs to be credible.
This req pairs market intelligence with discovery conversations, proposals, contract negotiation and roadmap influence. That is the exact pairing above: build the number, then carry it into the room where it gets tested.
