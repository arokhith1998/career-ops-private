Why this deck exists

A Global Strategy team does not lack data. It lacks time to turn competitor and financial files into one executive story before the deadline.

What a global strategy function runs into

Competitive intelligence across devices, pipelines, clinical evidence and go-to-market moves generates more raw signal than a small team can turn into a clean strategic narrative on a tight deadline.
Raw financial files (sales, pricing) and competitor data sit in different systems, so the real bottleneck is rarely the analysis itself. It is building one dashboard and one storyline that an executive audience can act on in a single read.
Annual and long-range planning cycles need that same loop repeated on a fixed calendar, across multiple franchises, without the narrative drifting between cycles.

What I have already run, at comparable scale

At Sensata, I built the Bleeders and Leakers Power BI dashboard: 1,200 opportunities ranked against a $627M target across 3 regions, 4 product families and 20 product lines, from raw sales and pricing files to one leadership view.
I benchmarked 10+ competitors to build the strategic narrative behind a +8% market share gain, then carried that narrative through AP26 revenue models with senior executives.
At Applied Materials now, I automate the recurring parts of that competitive and market-intelligence analysis with AI agents built on Claude, the same discipline a strategy team needs to keep pace between planning cycles.
This req asks for market insights, competitive analyses and a clear executive storyline, built on raw financial data and repeated across annual and long-range planning. That is the exact loop above: raw files in, one dashboard, one narrative, out.

First 30 days

What the first month on the Structural Heart Global Strategy team looks like.

Week 1
Map the existing signal
Pull the current competitive tracking, raw sales and pricing files, and the last planning cycle's executive deck. Sit with Commercial, Regulatory, Clinical and Finance to learn which data sources already feed the strategic narrative and which ones are still manual.
Why first: the same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers program, map what exists before building anything new.
Week 2-3
Build the first dashboard
Turn the highest-friction manual report into a Power BI view: competitor pipelines, clinical and regulatory milestones, and the raw financial files side by side, pressure-tested with Commercial on 1 to 2 live franchise questions.
Why now: building it against a real franchise question, not in a vacuum, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain at Sensata hold.
Week 4
Ship the executive narrative
Package the dashboard's findings into the concise, actionable executive presentation the strategic goals planning process runs on, with the data model documented so the next cycle starts from the same base instead of a blank slide.
Why this fast: a market-intelligence function only earns trust once a second team can run the model without you in the room.
