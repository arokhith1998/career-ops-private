At Sensata I built the FY2026 Bleeders and Leakers Power BI dashboard that gave leadership a live view of 1,200 deal-pricing opportunities against a $627M target, ranked by region and product line. The dashboard was the easy part. I recovered $700K+ of the margin it surfaced within two quarters by getting into live deals and renegotiating the structure myself, not stopping at the dashboard. That is the loop your posting describes for Deal Desk: catch the pattern in the deal flow, make it visible, then fix it at the source.

My read on deal desks is that most of them get judged on discount approvals when the real leverage is upstream, in the contract structure nobody checked before it hit Legal. When I repriced Sensata's distributor terms to lift margins from 53% to 61%, the number only held because Sales and Finance had agreed on the structure before it shipped, not after. I ran that same discipline on multimillion-dollar customer quotes with senior executives, where pricing, Finance and Legal had to line up before a deal moved, close to the land-expand-renewal judgment calls your team makes every week.

That is also why I started PriceKeel. I kept seeing discount exceptions get approved with no record of the reasoning behind them, so I built the audit trail that gives Finance real evidence for the call, and a guardrail engine that catches the exception before it becomes the new normal. I run the company's CRM, CPQ and contract approvals myself, the same playbook and approval-governance work that keeps a growing deal desk from becoming the bottleneck as deal volume scales.

I would bring that same approach, find the structural gap, fix it at the deal, document it so it holds, to Achievers' Deal Desk. Proof trail at adhi-pricing-ai.vercel.app/ref/achievers.
