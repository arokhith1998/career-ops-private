Why this deck exists

Calling something "agentic" does not make it durable. The question is who keeps it running after the first owner moves on.

What a GM-style B2B strategy seat runs into

A 0-1 bet gets scoped, piloted, and proven once, then the next team inherits a process that only worked because one person remembered every exception.
"Why isn't an agent doing this" is the right question to ask, but asking it does not replace the work of mapping which manual steps are actually safe to automate first.
The fix is not another pilot. It is an operator who ships the first version, writes the playbook, and makes the handoff boring on purpose.

What I have already run, hands on

At Sensata, I took the Bleeders and Leakers program from an ad-hoc negotiation habit to a repeatable process: a dashboard, a cadence, and a playbook other regions could run without me in the room.
At Applied Materials, I automate the recurring part of my own forecasting and market-sizing work with AI agents built on Claude, so the agent does the refresh and I do the judgment call.
At Pixis, I hired and ran a small team against an 8-client book with real P&L ownership, the same small cross-functional scope this role describes.
Incubate 0-1 bets, replace manual B2B GTM steps with agentic workflows, and turn pilots into playbooks other teams can run: this is the exact loop I already operate.

How a 0-1 bet gets to playbook, on a realistic clock

Your posting describes experiment, production, and playbook as three separate steps. Here is how I would run each one.

First Stretch
Pick the highest-leverage manual step, ship a working pilot
Partner with the GMs to find the single manual B2B step costing the most hours, ship a first working version with engineering, and define the metric that proves it is worth scaling.
Why this is realistic: it is the same opening move behind the Sensata dashboard: find the highest-leakage spot before building anything.
Second Stretch
Take the pilot to production, write the playbook
Harden the pilot with engineering so it runs safely and durably in production, then write the playbook and templates so a second team can run it without me in the room.
Why now: the same automate-then-document sequence that lets Claude agents run my recurring analysis at Applied Materials today.
Third Stretch
Make the win visible, make the next bet fundable
Establish the performance metrics leadership will trust, build the pitch deck and business case, and use that proof to win budget and backing for the next 0-1 bet.
Why this sticks: the same trust the Bleeders and Leakers program earned once it recovered $700K+ instead of just reporting a number.
