Hiring Team, Pomo

Pomo's pitch, one decision layer that consolidates planning and execution for marketing teams, is close to the exact problem I have been building for pricing teams at PriceKeel: a guardrail engine and audit trail that turns a live discount decision into one auditable record, replacing the patchwork of CPQ and deal-desk tools around it. I set up the company's CRM, pipeline reviews and deal approvals myself, I am running pilots with early customers, and I am raising the first round in parallel. The operating system this role asks for, planning cycles, key-result frameworks, one plan the whole team can point to, is the one I built for my own company from a blank page.

That same motion runs inside much larger organizations too. At Applied Materials I turn scattered input from Sales, Engineering and Accounts into one market-sizing view, and I run the OKR cadence behind weekly and quarterly business reviews while carrying P&L targets for the role. At Sensata I built a Power BI dashboard tracking 1,200 pricing opportunities against a $627M target, then used it to run the program that recovered $700K+ in margin in two quarters, working directly with senior executives on revenue models and site-transition strategy. That is the kind of complex, cross-functional initiative, spanning fundraising-adjacent strategy work and day-to-day execution, a CEO's office has to run without being asked twice.

I have also stood in the room representing that kind of office before it existed. On a pro-bono consulting engagement for Safran, I led a 5-consultant team that sized a market down from 170 facilities to a SAM of 28 and presented the pricing and market-entry recommendation to Safran's leadership directly. I have hired and managed teams end to end too, an 8-person account team at Pixis and the team behind my own SwipeHire product, so the people side of a founder's-office seat is not new territory.
