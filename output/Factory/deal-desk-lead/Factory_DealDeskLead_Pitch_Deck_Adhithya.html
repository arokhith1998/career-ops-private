Why this deck exists

Quote-to-booking at a fast-scaling AI company is three jobs held by one seat: deal review, CPQ ownership, and cross-functional governance.

What losing that discipline runs into

A deal desk without deal-by-deal review does not fail loudly. It fails as a quote booked on last quarter's discount logic, a CPQ workaround nobody documented, and a booking record Finance cannot trace back to its own approval.
Sales, Finance and Legal all touch the same quote on its way to booking, so a gap in CPQ ownership or booking data integrity becomes everyone's guessing game at close, not just one team's problem.
The fix is not another approval step. It is someone who treats quote review, system ownership and governance as one connected loop instead of three separate jobs.

What I have already run

At PriceKeel, I set up and run the CRM, CPQ configuration and the audit trail myself, the same system-ownership and governance loop this seat holds for Salesforce Revenue Cloud.
At Sensata, I reviewed deals for booking readiness and discount exceptions one by one inside the Bleeders and Leakers program, building the commercial terms behind multimillion-dollar customer quotes.
Both mean I have sat on the quote-approval side and the system-configuration side of the same gate, not just one or the other.
A Deal Desk Lead who owns quote-to-booking, administers Salesforce Revenue Cloud CPQ, ensures booking data integrity, and guides Sales toward higher-value deal structures is describing this exact loop: review the deal, configure the system, keep the record honest.
