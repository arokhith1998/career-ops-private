Why this deck exists

Onboarding stops scaling the moment it depends on a person instead of a product, and that moment looks like it has already arrived.

What happens without a single owner

A human-run onboarding motion scales with headcount, not with the product. As the surface area grows and new products launch, Customer Success keeps doing more individual hand-holding instead of the product doing more of the work.
Without one owner, Product, Customer Success and RevOps each define "onboarded" differently, and a Product Qualified Lead gets defined three separate times before anyone routes it.
The fix is not another dashboard. It is one person who treats discovery, the onboarding design and the adoption metric as a single job, not three handoffs.

What I have already run

At Pixis, I ran an 8-person Customer Success team across 8 accounts and cut churn 50% by building account strategy on actual usage data instead of renewal-date urgency, the same proactive, persona-deep relationship Ashby's CS team already owns.
At Sensata, I turned raw SQL and GA4 usage data into a KPI framework that told us which signals actually predicted adoption, then owned the 100+ feature roadmap built on top of it.
On SwipeHire, I owned onboarding from signup to first match alone, end to end, with no team to hand pieces to.
A single owner for the customer journey, who defines adoption metrics independently, partners tightly with Customer Success, and owns the Product Qualified Lead definition with RevOps, is describing a job I have done in pieces across three roles and now want to do as one.
