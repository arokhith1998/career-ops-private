Hiring Team, Snapmint

The line in your posting that stopped me was "no blockers." Most Founder's Office roles describe the projects; yours describes the failure mode, which is the more useful way to write it. At Sensata I owned the Bleeders and Leakers program as a standalone initiative and built the tracker behind it myself, ranking 1,200 pricing opportunities against a $627M target so leadership could see where the money was stuck instead of asking me for a status update. That turned into $700K+ recovered in two quarters. The habit is the same one your role is asking for: find the stuck thing, build the structure that shows it is stuck, then clear it.

I started PriceKeel this year, a B2B SaaS pricing company, and I am the one who runs the CRM and CPQ, the pipeline reviews and the deal approvals, because there is no one else to hand them to. That is a smaller version of what a founder's office does at a growing company: own two or three things end to end, keep your own metrics honest, and do not wait for someone to tell you a project has stalled.

The cross-functional half of the job is my day job right now too. At Applied Materials I sit between Sales, Engineering and Accounts and turn whatever each team hands me into one pricing and forecasting view, then automate the repetitive parts of that work with AI agents built on Claude so I can spend the saved hours on the next ambiguous ask instead of the last one. I have run that same loop since 2021: at Sensata on a multi-$100M business, at Plug Power managing a team and a six-figure monthly budget, and at Pixis running eight client P&Ls through one person.

Snapmint is scaling a zero-cost EMI business to 10 million consumers across 2,500 cities, which means the founder's priorities move fast and the person next to that work needs to move with them, not a cycle behind. I would like to be that person. Proof trail at adhithyabhaskar.vercel.app/ref/snapmint.
