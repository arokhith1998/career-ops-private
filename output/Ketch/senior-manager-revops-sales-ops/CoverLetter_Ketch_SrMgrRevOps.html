Hiring Team, Ketch

At Sensata I built the FY2026 Bleeders and Leakers Power BI dashboard that gave leadership visibility into 1,200 pricing opportunities against a $627M target, ranked by win rate, dollar-per-win and stage across three regions. The dashboard was the easy half. The $700K+ I recovered in the two quarters after came from sitting in the deal room and renegotiating the structures the dashboard flagged, not just reporting the gap up the chain. That is the loop your posting describes for the Sales Ops seat: own the pricing call and the approval, not just the report on it.

My opinion on deal desk work is that most of it fails at the handoff, not the judgment. A rep can usually explain why a discount makes sense. What actually breaks is the system around it: no CPQ record of why the exception got approved, no forecast that reflects the deal once it closes, no one person who owns both sides of that gap. I started PriceKeel because I kept watching that exact failure play out, and I now run the company's CRM, CPQ, forecast reviews and deal approvals myself, as the only ops person in the building. That is closer to what "primary ops resource" means in practice than most job posts let on.

The same pattern shows up everywhere I have worked since. At Sensata I carried P&L and revenue targets for a multi-$100M business unit and raised distributor and channel-partner margins from 53% to 61%, not by handing out bigger discounts but by rebuilding the deal structures with Sales and Finance until they held. At Applied Materials I automate the recurring pricing and forecasting analysis with Claude agents, because a lean ops function only scales if the repeatable parts run themselves and the person doing it can still think about the next deal.

I would bring that same habit, own the system and the deal, to Ketch's Sales Ops seat. Proof trail at adhi-pricing-ai.vercel.app/ref/ketch.
