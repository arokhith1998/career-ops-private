Why this deck exists

A founder's first Chief of Staff hire is not a reporting job. It is a bet that one person can run GTM execution end to end without being told how.

What the seat actually demands

Partner directly with the founder, then drive execution across sales, marketing, partnerships and operations: four functions that do not report to the Chief of Staff, moved by one owned number instead of four separate updates.
Analyze metrics and turn them into data-driven recommendations, with dashboards and an operating cadence that keep the founder's priorities visible between the big meetings, not just during them.
Own special projects from ideation to implementation: the role is judged on whether the project shipped, not on whether it was well scoped.

First 30 days

What the first month as this founder's Chief of Staff looks like.

Week 1
Map GTM end to end
Sit with sales, marketing, partnerships and operations to hear where the GTM number is stuck, which special projects are already in flight, and what the founder actually needs reported weekly versus monthly.
Why first: the same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers dashboard at Sensata. The gap is usually visible before anyone has framed it as one problem.
Week 2-3
Build the dashboard and the cadence
Turn the GTM metrics into one dashboard, set the weekly operating cadence around it, and pick the first special project to run end to end alongside the functions that have to execute it.
Why now: the dashboard came before the opinion at Sensata, and the fix held because the cadence around it did too.
Week 4
Ship the first special project
Carry that first project from ideation to implementation, report the outcome against the dashboard, and hand the founder a repeatable process for the next one instead of a one-off fix.
Why this fast: a Chief of Staff earns the mandate by shipping something the founder can point to, not by proposing a plan.
