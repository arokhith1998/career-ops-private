Your posting asks for someone who can "identify new pricing models while understanding the complexity and implications to existing base." That sentence described my last two quarters at Sensata almost exactly: I built the Bleeders and Leakers program, a Power BI system that ranked 1,200 pricing opportunities against a $627M target across 3 regions and 4 product families, surfaced $433M in margin gaps, and then recovered $700K+ of it by turning competitive and customer-willingness-to-pay data into recommendations that Sales and Finance could actually act on without breaking what was already working for existing customers.

That program only worked because it was built the way your Experience section describes: hypothesis, data, validation, recommendation. Every repricing move started with a question (where is this distributor's margin wrong relative to what they would actually pay), got tested against real deal data, then went to the people who would have to defend it. The distributor and channel-partner repricing alone raised margins from 53% to 61% and held, because Sales and Finance signed off on the model before it shipped, not after. I carried P&L and revenue targets for that Industrial BU the entire time, and I partnered directly with senior executives on AP26 revenue models and multimillion-dollar customer quotes, so communicating a pricing recommendation to EVP/SVP stakeholders in language they can act on is not new ground for me.

At Applied Materials now, I run a version of the same loop at a different kind of complexity: partnering with Sales, Engineering and Accounts to turn field and technical input into one pricing strategy across multiple product groups, forecasting the market's inflection points instead of pricing a quarter behind them. What I would bring to a Pricing Strategy seat at Salesforce's scale is the same instinct applied to a much larger and more varied product portfolio: SKUs and packaging that map cleanly to what a product actually does, instead of pricing logic that drifts apart from the product roadmap over time.

The other thing I would bring is that I am currently building the tool a pricing organization at this scale eventually wants internally. PriceKeel is a decision-integrity layer for B2B SaaS deal pricing, in pilots now, that gives Finance an audit trail for every discount exception instead of a judgment call nobody can reconstruct later. Designing that guardrail logic from scratch is the same work as crafting a new pricing model for Salesforce's own portfolio: you have to understand exactly how a change ripples through the deals that already exist before you ship it.
