Why this deck exists

The problem is not that Salesforce lacks pricing data. It is that every new SKU has to earn its place without breaking the base that already exists.

What a widening portfolio runs into

CRM, Agentforce and a growing set of clouds get sold on the same account, often the same quote, so a new pricing model or SKU has to make sense next to every price already on the books.
A packaging decision made in isolation from Product, GTM, Sales and Deal Desk becomes a renegotiation six months later, and that renegotiation costs more than getting the SKU right the first time.
The fix is not more market research in a vacuum. It is a structured hypothesis-test-recommend process that reaches an EVP/SVP decision before the SKU ships, not after a quarter of customer confusion.

What I have already run, at smaller scale

At Sensata, I led global pricing strategy for a multi-$100M Industrial BU across multiple product lines at once, the same "one pricing logic across many lines" problem.
I ran the Bleeders and Leakers program: a Power BI system that ranked pricing opportunities by region and product family against a hard revenue target, so leadership knew exactly where to act and why.
I am now building PriceKeel, a tool that gives Finance an audit trail for every discount decision, because a pricing change that cannot be explained to the existing base is the change that gets reversed.
A Pricing Strategy Manager/Senior Manager who develops long-term pricing and packaging strategies, makes data-driven recommendations and nurtures EVP/SVP relationships is describing this exact loop: one pricing logic across a growing portfolio, proven with a model, and held together across teams.

First 30 days

What the first month on Salesforce's Pricing Strategy team looks like.

Week 1
Map the portfolio and the base
Pull the last 90 days of packaging and SKU decisions across the products this seat touches. Sit with Product Management, Product Marketing, GTM, Sales and Deal Desk to hear where a new SKU already strained an existing customer's contract.
Why first: the leak is usually visible in the data before anyone has named it out loud. Same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers program.
Week 2-3
Build the hypothesis and validate it
Pick one pricing or packaging question with real exec visibility, form a hypothesis, test it against customer and competitive research, then pressure-test the recommendation with Sales and Deal Desk before it reaches EVP/SVP leadership.
Why now: hypothesis-test-recommend, then verify it cross-functionally, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain stick.
Week 4
Make the recommendation repeatable
Turn the first recommendation into a template other product lines can reuse: a competitive-research view, a validation checklist and an EVP/SVP-ready narrative for the next pricing or packaging decision.
Why this fast: a pricing recommendation matters less if it only works once. Repeatability is the real ask behind "cross-functional expertise."
