Why this deck exists

The posting is direct: dedicated capacity for pricing and order operations testing disappeared, and QTC changes started shipping unvalidated.

What losing that capacity runs into

A pricing or order change that skips UAT and regression testing does not fail loudly. It fails three weeks later as a broken booking, a signature packet that will not route, or a marketplace offer that quotes the wrong number.
Order Operations, IT's business systems team, Pricing Operations, Sales and Deal Desk all touch the same QTC path, so a gap in testing capacity becomes everyone's fire drill, not just one team's.
The fix is not more Salesforce CPQ configuration. It is someone who treats every release as a test case first and documents the system so the next change does not repeat the same fire drill.

What I have already run, at smaller scale

At PriceKeel, I built the company's own CRM, CPQ and quote-to-cash workflow from deal approval through order documentation, then set up the testing and audit trail Finance reviews before anything ships.
At Sensata, I built the Bleeders and Leakers Power BI system that validated 1,200 pricing opportunities against a $627M target, the same discipline of checking a change against a hard number before trusting it.
Both mean I have sat on the same side of the table as Order Operations, Pricing Operations and Deal Desk, not just adjacent to it, coordinating a testable, documented change with all three at once.
A Pricing & Order Operations Analyst who validates QTC changes, executes UAT, and maintains the regression suite and system documentation, is describing this exact loop: test before it ships, document why, and keep it repeatable.
