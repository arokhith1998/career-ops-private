Why this deck exists

A planning seat like this does not fail on effort. It fails when the model and the narrative built on top of it stop agreeing with each other.

What a Growth org at this stage runs into

Target setting, budget allocation and pipeline coverage all run on the same assumptions, so a planning model built from ambiguous inputs has to be defensible the first time it meets Finance and a VP, not refined over three review cycles.
The risk is not a missing dashboard. It is a model whose coverage, conversion and velocity assumptions quietly drift from what Field Ops and Finance believe, until a business review surfaces the gap in front of leadership instead of before it.
The fix is a model built to be interrogated: one owner, one set of assumptions, reconciled with Finance and Field Ops before the narrative goes to the VP.

What I have already run, at comparable scale

At Sensata, I designed a Power BI model ranking 1,200 opportunities against a $627M target across 3 regions and 20 product lines, then defended its own recommendations to senior executives until they became $700K+ in recovered margin.
I did not stop at the model. I reconciled it against real deal outcomes every quarter, which is why the recommendations held up past the first review.
I run the same coverage-and-conversion discipline today at PriceKeel: forecast and pipeline reviews, deal approvals, and an audit trail Finance can check the assumptions against.
Target setting, budget allocation, and a commercial pipeline model reconciled with Field Ops and Finance are describing this exact loop: build the model once, defend its assumptions, keep it honest as the business target moves.
