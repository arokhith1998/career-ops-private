Why this deck exists

Four teams touch every pricing change. Without one enablement timeline, the field hears it four different ways, or not at all.

What happens without a single enablement timeline

Pricing, Product, Deal Desk and Product Marketing each move at their own pace. When nobody owns the single timeline across all four, sellers get a packaging update from one team and a consumption clarification from another, days apart and half-contradicting.
A rep who was not told a new ELA requires approval quotes it the old way, the deal stalls in Deal Desk, and the pricing team hears about the gap only after the escalation.
The fix is not another slide deck pushed out once. It is a cadence that catches the change before it ships, and a reinforcement loop that catches the miss after it does.

What I have already run, at smaller scale

At Sensata, I turned a distributor repricing decision into guidance 50+ reps could use immediately, briefing the field and confirming the quoting workflow matched the new policy before the first deal closed under it.
At PriceKeel, I built the guardrail that flags a pricing exception before it reaches a signed quote, plus the audit trail that tells Finance and RevOps why a deal was approved the way it was.
Both mean I have already sat where this seat sits: between the team that changes the policy and the rep who has to sell under it, on the same day.
A Senior Pricing Enablement Manager who owns one enablement timeline across Pricing, Product, Deal Desk and Product Marketing, and reinforces it with field feedback until behavior actually changes, is describing this exact loop.
