Why this deck exists

The problem is not that Adyen lacks pricing insight. It is that a region sells local markets, products and payment methods at once, and a global price guide cannot see all three.

What pricing one region on a global platform runs into

A price that reflects value in one local market reads as a discount or an overcharge in the next, so "set the best possible price" has to be answered market by market, not once for the whole region.
The commercial organization, Sales, account management, product management and Finance, all touch the same deal, so a pricing initiative only sticks if every one of them can act on it, not just read it.
A global pricing tool only earns regional adoption once it saves someone a real decision. Feedback that never reaches the global team gets rebuilt locally instead of fixed once.

What I have already run, at regional scale

At Sensata, I led pricing strategy for a multi-$100M Industrial BU across 3 regions and 4 product families at once, the same "one pricing logic, several local realities" problem.
I ran the Bleeders and Leakers program: a Power BI system that ranked pricing opportunities by region and product family against a hard revenue target, so regional leadership knew exactly where to act.
I am now building PriceKeel, a tool that gives Finance an audit trail for every discount decision, because a pricing team only trusts a tool it can explain to the person who approved the exception.
A Strategic Pricing Manager who develops pricing for local markets, products and payment methods, partners with regional leadership and stakeholders, and drives adoption of global pricing tools, is describing this exact loop: one pricing logic, several local realities, held together by a tool people actually use.

First 30 days

What the first month as Adyen's North America pricing expert looks like.

Week 1
Map the region's pricing surface
Pull the last quarter's deal pricing requests and discount exceptions by local market, product and payment method. Sit with Sales, account management and Finance to hear where the global price guide breaks down on a regional deal.
Why first: the leak is usually visible in the data before anyone has named it out loud. Same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers program.
Week 2-3
Model and pressure-test
Build a value-based pricing model for the region's top 3 pricing questions, pressure-test it with regional leadership and the commercial team, then ship one pricing initiative with a documented, cross-functionally-agreed reason.
Why now: modeling against a hard target, then verifying the fix cross-functionally, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain stick.
Week 4
Channel feedback, drive tool adoption
Turn the one-off fix into a template the region reuses, and send what the global pricing tool is missing back to the global team instead of letting the region rebuild its own workaround.
Why this fast: "drive adoption while channeling regional feedback" only works if both directions move in the first month, not just one.
