Why this deck exists

The risk is not a slow forecast. It is Finance, ProServe and the account team each holding a slightly different number for the same quarter.

What a complex, high-touch segment runs into

Strategic Accounts is a portfolio of AWS's largest, most scrutinized relationships, which means the cost of a stale stage definition or an un-reconciled variance shows up fastest in a WBR or MBR, in front of the people who act on the number.
As territory, quota and account plans shift under a segment this size, pipeline hygiene and forecast accuracy drift apart unless one shared standard is forcing Finance, ProServe and the account team to read the same definitions.
The fix is not another reconciliation meeting. It is a documented metrics mechanism, plus the change-management habit that keeps the standard from decaying as the account base grows.

What I have already run, at comparable scale

At Sensata, I built the Bleeders and Leakers Power BI dashboard: 1,200 opportunities scored against a $627M target across 3 regions and 20 product lines, with one win-rate and stage-distribution definition every stakeholder used.
I did not stop at the dashboard. I resolved the variance behind the worst gaps directly with the account owners, which is why the $433M in surfaced margin turned into $700K+ actually recovered in 2 quarters.
I run that same loop today at PriceKeel: CRM and CPQ, forecast and pipeline reviews, and deal approvals, with a documented audit trail, because a metrics mechanism only works if someone owns it after launch.
Revenue analysis and forecast accuracy, pipeline quality and hygiene, and goals-and-metrics mechanisms for accountability are describing this exact loop: name the variance once, document the standard, and keep Finance, ProServe and the account team reading the same number.

First 30 days

What the first month on AWS Strategic Accounts' Business Operations team looks like.

Week 1
Map where the numbers disagree
Pull stage definitions, forecast inputs and account plans as they exist today across Finance, ProServe and the account teams, and trace where two groups use the same term to mean different things in a WBR or MBR.
Why first: the disagreement is visible in the data before anyone names it. Same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers program.
Week 2-3
Ship the metrics mechanism
Document one forecast-accuracy and pipeline-hygiene standard, build the self-service dashboard behind it, and pressure-test it with Finance, ProServe and account leadership against real territory and quota plans before it goes into the weekly cadence.
Why now: naming the variance against one real target, then verifying with every stakeholder, is what made the $433M figure turn into $700K+ recovered.
Week 4
Standardize and train on it
Turn the Week 1 findings into a change-management and training standard for the account teams, define the tooling requirements the standard exposes, and set the first AI-tooling use case with its impact measured, not assumed.
Why this fast: a metrics mechanism matters less if the training and tooling behind it stay ad hoc. Repeatability is the actual ask behind accountability.
