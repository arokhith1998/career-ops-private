Why this deck exists

The role is not "write down the process." It is making a standardized process, a shared set of KPI definitions, and a new AI tool all survive contact with a fast-growing sales org at once.

What that actually requires

A documented process only works if the people running quota against it agree on what each KPI means, or a business review turns into an argument about definitions instead of performance.
Pipeline qualification and attribution has to be resolved consistently, or the same deal gets counted twice, or not at all, and forecast accuracy erodes from the bottom up.
AI sales intelligence tools only help if sellers actually adopt them, which is a training and change-management problem sitting right next to the technical rollout, not after it.

What I have already run

At Sensata, I built the KPI definitions and the Power BI dashboard a whole business unit ran its weekly and quarterly reviews on, across 3 regions and 20 product lines.
At PriceKeel, the company I founded, I run CRM, CPQ, forecast and pipeline reviews, and deal approvals myself, with an audit trail Finance can check the human decision against.
At Sensata again, I built the sales enablement that got 50-plus reps onto a new pitch, and at Applied Materials today I run my own analysis on AI agents built on Claude.
The through-line: every process I have standardized had to hold up under a live business review with real revenue behind it, and every tool I have rolled out had to get adopted, not just documented.

First 30 days

What the first month inside ASP's sales operations looks like.

Week 1
Map today's KPI definitions and business review flow
Sit with the ASP leaders and sellers who run today's business reviews, and document where a metric means one thing in one team's deck and something else in another's, before touching any tooling.
Why first: the same first move behind the Sensata dashboard, listening to how a number actually gets used before redefining it.
Week 2-3
Standardize one pipeline qualification and attribution rule
Pick the qualification or attribution rule causing the most escalations, resolve it with Finance and the sellers who feel it, and document it as the KPI definition and goal cascade the next business review runs on.
Why now: the same standardize-until-the-number-moves habit that raised Sensata's channel margin from 53% to 61%.
Week 4
Pair one AI sales intelligence tool with an adoption plan
Take one AI-driven tool already in flight for ASP, build the training and workflow automation that gets sellers actually using it, and measure adoption the same way I measured rep pitch adoption at Sensata.
Why this fast: the same habit that keeps my own reporting moving on AI agents at Applied Materials today, not a plan for someday.
