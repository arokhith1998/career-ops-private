Why this deck exists

A Pricing Committee does not lack analysis. It lacks a documentation system that survives the handoff to SFDC/CPQ and sales enablement.

What a SaaS pricing function runs into

Pricing decisions get made in committee, but the documentation that is supposed to be the source of truth drifts the moment a change has to cascade into CPQ, the website and sales enablement.
Discounting analysis and packaging optimization work are only useful if a Product Manager can act on structured, direct feedback instead of a vague steer, and if Sales and Deal Desk get a fast, consistent answer on pricing Q&A.
Across SaaS, AI and Contact Center pricing, the hard part is rarely the model. It is keeping governance, systems and committee process in sync as the business moves.

What I have already run, at comparable scale

At PriceKeel, I designed a guardrail engine and an audit trail Finance can review for every discount exception, and set up the CRM, CPQ and deal-approval flow it runs against.
At Sensata, I ran the Bleeders and Leakers program end to end: discounting analysis on 1,200 opportunities against a $627M target, recovering $700K+ in two quarters and lifting distributor margins 53% to 61%.
At Applied Materials now, I build competitive, value-based pricing and automate the recurring parts of that analysis with AI agents built on Claude, keeping documentation current between cycles.
This req asks for Pricing Committee support, documentation as source of truth, discounting analysis and Deal Desk Q&A. That is the exact governance loop above, run on both an industrial book of business and inside a pricing-native startup.
