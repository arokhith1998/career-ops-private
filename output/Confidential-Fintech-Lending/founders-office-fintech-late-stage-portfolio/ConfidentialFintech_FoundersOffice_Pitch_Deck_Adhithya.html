Why this deck exists

A Founders' Office role is not a staff job. It is a bet that one person can turn a fuzzy, high-stakes problem into an owned outcome.

What that bet actually requires

No fixed job description: the mandate shifts to whatever the CEO's highest-priority problem is this quarter, which means the person has to build credibility with Sales, Credit, Operations and Finance fast, with no org-chart authority over any of them.
The scoring criteria is not "did you analyze it well." It is "did the metric move," which means owning a plan through execution and reporting, not handing a deck back up the chain.
As the company moves toward a public listing, the same person is expected to help institutionalize the reporting rigor and operating cadence that a growing, IPO-track business needs to run on.

What I have already run, at founder scale

I run PriceKeel myself: product, CRM/CPQ, forecast and pipeline reviews, deal approvals, and the investor-facing narrative for the round I am currently raising. Nobody assigned me that scope.
At Sensata, I ran the Bleeders and Leakers program end to end: found $433M in margin gaps, then closed $700K+ of it in 2 quarters through negotiations with Sales and Finance I had no authority to compel.

The through-line across every role on my resume is the same one this posting is describing: take a cross-functional, ambiguous problem, build the plan, run it, and report the outcome, without waiting for someone to grant the authority first.

First 30 days

What the first month inside a Founders' Office looks like.

Week 1
Map the problem, not the org chart
Sit with the founders and the functions closest to the current priority problem (product, credit, sales, operations, finance) to hear where the metric is stuck and who already has partial context on why.
Why first: the same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers program. The gap is usually visible in the data and in people's frustration before anyone has framed it as one problem.
Week 2-3
Build the plan, then earn the alignment
Turn the problem into a structured plan with a number attached, pressure-test it with the functions that have to execute it, and start moving the metric before asking for a formal mandate to own it.
Why now: authority followed results at Sensata and follows results at PriceKeel. Alignment earned through a credible plan moves faster than alignment requested through a title.
Week 4
Institutionalize the cadence
Turn the one-off fix into a repeatable operating rhythm: an OKR or business-review cadence, a reporting format, and a clear owner, so the next priority problem has a process to plug into instead of starting cold.
Why this fast: reporting rigor that only exists in one person's head does not scale toward a public listing. Repeatability is the actual ask behind "process institutionalization."
