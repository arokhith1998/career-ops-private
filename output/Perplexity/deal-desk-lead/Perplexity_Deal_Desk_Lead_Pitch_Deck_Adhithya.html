Why this deck exists

The problem is not that Perplexity lacks a pricing policy. It is that a Deal Desk function built from zero has to keep pace with how fast an AI-native company closes deals, without becoming the thing Sales routes around.

What building a Deal Desk from zero actually requires

An approval framework and quote-to-order workflow that Sales, Finance and Legal all trust enough to move fast on, not a policy document nobody opens before a live call.
A way to model each deal's economics, discounts, commitments and payment terms, that an account executive can get a workable alternative from in minutes, not a flat yes or no after escalation.
Business requirements and acceptance criteria for the quote-to-order system itself, with someone willing to run business acceptance testing and sign off, not just configure a tool someone else specced.

What I have already run

At PriceKeel, I built the discount-guardrail engine and Finance audit trail from zero, the exact approval-framework-plus-audit-trail this seat stands up for Perplexity.
At Sensata, I recovered $700K+ in margin in 2 quarters negotiating nonstandard terms deal by deal, and closed AP26 revenue models and multimillion-dollar customer quotes directly with senior executives.
I own my own CRM, CPQ, forecast and pipeline reviews and quote-to-order requirements at PriceKeel instead of inheriting someone else's stack, the same build-from-zero mandate this role carries.
The through-line: every Deal Desk system I have touched started with a nonstandard request that needed a fast, defensible call, and ended with a rule Legal, Finance and Sales could all act on without waiting on me.
