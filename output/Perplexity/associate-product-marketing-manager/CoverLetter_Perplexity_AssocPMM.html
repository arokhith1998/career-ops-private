Hiring Team, Perplexity

Your posting says the job is to know the product better than the team that built it, then turn that into something a new user can act on in one sentence. At Sensata I did the second half of that loop for a hardware line most marketers would have handed straight to engineering: I turned sensing-product specs into positioning that lifted conversion 15% on priority launches, then built the case studies and sales decks that took 50 reps from "reading the spec sheet" to a 30% higher win rate. Clarity was the product; the launch was just where it got tested.

The interviews came first, not after. I ran 30+ conversations with OEMs and distributors to find exactly where they got stuck before I wrote a word of messaging, and that habit is why CSAT moved 22% on the launches that followed. I read your posting's line about staying grounded in how people actually use the product, not how you assume they do, as the same discipline: sit with confused users before you write the explanation, not after.

I also don't treat education as a content deliverable that ships once. I built the KPI framework in SQL and GA4 that tracked adoption signals across 50,000+ connected devices, so I could see which explanations were actually landing and which weren't, and adjust before the quarter closed rather than after. That is the "measure outcomes, not just outputs" instinct your posting asks for, applied to a webinar series or a how-to library instead of a dashboard.

Right now, as Strategic Marketing III at Applied Materials, I automate the recurring parts of my own analysis with AI agents built on Claude, freeing the hours for the work that actually needs a human explaining it well. That's the same trade I'd make running your product education engine: spend less time producing the tenth version of a guide and more time in the room with users who are still stuck.
