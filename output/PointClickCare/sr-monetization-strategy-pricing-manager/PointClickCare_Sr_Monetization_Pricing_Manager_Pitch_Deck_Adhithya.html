Why this deck exists

The problem is not that PointClickCare lacks pricing data. It is that packaging one platform across many provider segments multiplies every pricing decision.

What pricing across many segments and partners runs into

Post-acute and long-term care providers range from small operators to large enterprise groups, so one packaging and discounting framework has to flex without turning into a different deal every time.
A 400+ partner Marketplace means a new-product monetization call also touches partner economics, not just the core platform price, so the math has more moving pieces than a single-SKU product.
Without one pricing logic that Product, Sales, RevOps and Finance all hold to, running AI tools faster through pricing models just produces a worse decision faster, not a better one.

What I have already run, at smaller scale

At Sensata, I led global pricing and packaging strategy for a multi-$100M Industrial BU across multiple product lines at once, the same many-segments-one-logic problem.
I ran the Bleeders and Leakers program: a Power BI system that ranked pricing opportunities by region and product family against a hard revenue target, so leadership knew exactly where to act.
I am building PriceKeel now: an audit trail that gives Finance evidence for every discount decision, because the leak comes back if nobody can explain why the last exception was approved.
A Senior Monetization Strategy & Pricing Manager who develops pricing and packaging strategy, monetizes new products, and tracks ASP, discount rate, attach rate and margin impact with AI tools across Product, Marketing, Sales, RevOps and Finance, is describing this exact loop: one pricing logic for many segments and partners, proven with a model, held together across teams.

First 30 days

What the first month on PointClickCare's monetization and pricing desk looks like.

Week 1
Map the packaging landscape
Pull the last 90 days of discount exceptions and new-feature monetization asks across provider segments and the Marketplace. Sit with Product, Sales and Finance to hear where one packaging rule breaks down on a mixed-segment deal.
Why first: the leak is usually visible in the data before anyone has named it out loud. Same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers program.
Week 2-3
Build the AI-assisted model and pressure-test
Build a financial model tracking ASP, discount rate, attach rate and margin impact for one real pricing or packaging question, then pressure-test it with Sales and RevOps before proposing a fix with a documented, agreed reason.
Why now: modeling against a hard target, then verifying the fix cross-functionally, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain stick.
Week 4
Make it repeatable
Turn the one-off fix into a template: a discounting framework, a sales-enablement one-pager, and a KPI dashboard Sales and Finance both sign off on before the next packaging question comes in.
Why this fast: pricing strategy matters less if it only works once. Repeatability is the actual ask behind "support the Director in broader strategic initiatives."
