Why this deck exists

Pricing enablement is not a title, it is a maintenance job. The dashboard and the calendar have to survive after the strategy meeting ends.

What a pricing-enablement seat runs into

A markdown or promotion decision is easy to approve once. It is hard to make the SAME way every week, across Merchandising, Planning, Store Operations and Visual Merchandising, without a process and a calendar behind it.
Exceptions pile up faster than anyone can review them by hand, and without a scored system, the biggest margin leaks hide behind the smallest-looking line items.
A price-testing agenda only works if someone owns turning test results into an actual pricing action, not just a readout.

What I have already built, at comparable scale

At Sensata, I built the Bleeders & Leakers Power BI dashboard that scored 1,200 pricing opportunities against a $627M target, so every exception had a rank, not a guess.
I rolled that process out through distribution partners and trained the team on it, lifting margins 53% to 61%, not a one-time win.
At PriceKeel, I build guardrail logic that resolves a pricing exception against the company's own closed-won and closed-lost history, the same root-cause discipline a markdown-exception queue needs.
This req asks for pricing-process and calendar ownership, exception and root-cause resolution, a price-testing agenda, cross-functional coordination, and tool and dashboard build-out. That is the exact loop above, run at a $627M scale.

First 30 days

What the first month owning Athleta's pricing process and calendar looks like.

Week 1
Map the markdown calendar and exception queue
Sit with Merchandising, Planning and Store Operations to build one view of the current promo and markdown calendar and where exceptions pile up, the same first move that built the Bleeders and Leakers dashboard at Sensata.
Why first: a pricing process only holds if the people running it agree on one shared calendar.
Week 2-3
Score the first exception backlog by root cause
Pressure-test 2 to 3 live markdown or promotion exceptions with Visual Merchandising and tech partners, and draft the first price-testing agenda item ready to run.
Why now: scoring by root cause, not reviewing case by case, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain hold at Sensata.
Week 4
Ship the first tool and training rollout
Automate one recurring piece of the pricing-exception workflow, and run the first training session so the process keeps running without me in the room.
Why this fast: the same habit that keeps Claude agents running recurring pricing analysis at Applied Materials today, checked before every output ships.
