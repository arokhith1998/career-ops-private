Why this deck exists

A Pricing Strategy team does not lack pricing questions. It lacks the financial model and the cross-functional alignment to turn one into execution.

What a cross-functional pricing function runs into

A pricing recommendation is only as good as the financial model behind it: the impact on revenue, margin and user behavior has to be defensible before Finance, Legal, Product and GTM will align on it.
Pricing products as they evolve means the team's own process has to scale too, with consistent, visible, repeatable work instead of a one-off analysis each time.
Stakeholder engagement is the actual bottleneck more often than the analysis itself: driving alignment across functions on a commercially visible decision takes real operational rigor.

First 30 days

What the first month on Stripe's Pricing Strategy team looks like.

Week 1
Learn how a pricing decision moves today
Sit with Product, Finance, Legal and Go-to-Market to map how a recent pricing proposal moved from insight to execution, and where the process already has structure versus where it is still ad hoc.
Why first: the same move that started the PriceKeel audit trail, map what is missing before building anything new.
Week 2-3
Build the first financial model
Take one live pricing or packaging question and build the model evaluating its revenue, margin and user-behavior impact, pressure-tested with Finance and Product before it reaches a proposal.
Why now: building it against a real question, not in a vacuum, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain at Sensata hold.
Week 4
Ship a scalable process piece
Turn the most recurring, least consistent step in that process into a tool or template the team can reuse, so the next pricing proposal starts from structure instead of a blank page.
Why this fast: a Pricing Strategy function only scales once a second person can run the model without you in the room.
