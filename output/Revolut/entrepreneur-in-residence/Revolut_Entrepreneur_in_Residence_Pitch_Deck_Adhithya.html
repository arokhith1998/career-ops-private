Why this deck exists

An Entrepreneur in Residence has to do two things a normal product hire does not: start something from nothing, and fix something that already exists.

What the seat actually demands

Developing a new product from inception is a different skill than running an established roadmap: there is no backlog to inherit, no existing revenue model, and no team until someone builds one.
Revitalizing an underperforming initiative is the harder twin problem: the team, the roadmap and the revenue model already exist, and the job is to find where the case for change is strongest before touching anything.
Both need the same person to own the full P&L, from strategy and revenue modeling through growth optimization, and to direct a team and a set of cross-functional partners through it.

What I have already run, on both sides of that problem

At PriceKeel, I am building a product from inception right now: designed the guardrail engine and audit trail, set up the CRM, CPQ and deal-approval process from zero, and I am running pilots while raising the company's first round.
At Sensata, I took an underperforming margin line and turned it into the Bleeders and Leakers program, ranking 1,200 pricing opportunities against a $627M target and negotiating $700K plus of that back into margin in two quarters.
Both required the same discipline: revenue modeling grounded in real numbers, a team pointed at the highest-leverage fix, and a P&L that moved because of it.
A candidate who has founded a company and revitalized a stalled revenue line inside a large company is not two different profiles. It is one operating pattern, applied to a founding problem and a fixing problem, which is exactly the range this role asks for.

First 30 days

What the first month as Revolut's Entrepreneur in Residence looks like.

Week 1
Map the candidate initiatives
Sit with Design, Engineering, Marketing, Legal and FinCrime to hear which new product ideas and underperforming initiatives are already on the table, and where each one's revenue model and ownership currently stand.
Why first: the strongest opportunity is usually visible in what already exists. Same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers program at Sensata.
Week 2-3
Build the P&L and the team
Turn the strongest opportunity into a revenue model and a growth plan, then build or direct the cross-functional team needed to run it, pressure-testing assumptions with Legal and FinCrime before scaling spend.
Why now: building the model with the real stakeholders in the room, not in a vacuum, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain stick.
Week 4
Ship and instrument it
Launch the first version, put the OKR cadence and reporting rhythm in place, and set the growth-optimization loop that lets the initiative run without a founder in every meeting.
Why this fast: a product only earns the name "owned" once it can run a full lifecycle without you in the room.
