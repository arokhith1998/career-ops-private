Why this deck exists

A security portfolio this size does not have a pricing-data problem. It has a willingness-to-pay problem.

What a portfolio like this runs into

Hardware, software and services sold across multiple licensing and consumption models mean elasticity looks different in every segment, and a single discount KPI dashboard cannot say why.
Annual pricing reviews surface the symptom (margin drift, a discount creeping up) without always surfacing the underlying willingness-to-pay or competitive shift that caused it.
The fix is not another chart. It is a repeatable way to test elasticity, read the competitive and market signal, and turn both into pricing logic the team can defend.

What I have already run, at smaller scale

At Sensata, I ran the Bleeders and Leakers program: willingness-to-pay and competitive-gap data ranked against a hard revenue target, by region and product line.
I tested and refined the underlying pricing logic itself, which is why the 53 percent to 61 percent distributor gain held past the first quarter instead of drifting back.
I am now building PriceKeel, a subscription and consumption deal-pricing product, so I have run the elasticity exercise on both a hardware BU and a SaaS pricing model.
Support pricing and monetization strategy, analyze buyer behavior and willingness to pay, build scalable pricing tools, monitor discount KPIs. That is the same loop I ran at Sensata, just applied to a smaller portfolio than Cisco's.

First 30 days

What the first month on this pricing desk looks like.

Week 1
Read the elasticity signal
Pull the last pricing review's discount KPIs and competitive data by product line and licensing model. Sit with the pricing and finance teams to find where willingness-to-pay data is thin or contradicts the current discount guardrails.
Why first: the same move that opened the Bleeders and Leakers program: find where the data is quiet before proposing a fix.
Week 2-3
Build and test the pricing tool
Build or extend a scalable pricing model that ranks opportunities against the elasticity and competitive data, then test one pricing-logic change against a single product line before scaling it.
Why now: ranking against a hard signal, then testing before scaling, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain hold.
Week 4
Ship the KPI view
Turn the test into a discount KPI view the team monitors on a recurring cadence, with the elasticity and willingness-to-pay logic documented well enough to survive the next annual pricing review.
Why this fast: a pricing tool only earns its name if it gets monitored after the first win, not just built once.
