Why this deck exists

The problem is not that Cisco lacks pricing data. It is that margin leakage hides inside scale.

What a portfolio this size runs into

Thousands of SKUs across hardware, software and services, sold direct and through a large partner and distributor network, mean pricing exceptions and discount requests come from every direction at once.
Every exception that gets approved without a clear, auditable reason becomes a small margin leak, and small leaks compound fast across a portfolio this size.
The fix is not more dashboards. It is a repeatable way to spot the leak, explain it in the language Sales and Finance both accept, and make the correction hold.

What I have already run, at smaller scale

At Sensata, I ran the Bleeders and Leakers program: a Power BI system that ranked pricing opportunities against a hard revenue target, by region and product line, so leadership knew exactly where to act.
I did not stop at finding the leak. I repriced the underlying distributor logic, which is why the 53 percent to 61 percent margin gain held past the first quarter.
I am now building PriceKeel, a tool that gives Finance an audit trail for every discount decision, because the leak usually comes back if nobody can explain why the last exception was approved.
A Pricing Business Analyst role that develops creative solutions to pricing and business challenges, and drives cross-functional margin and process improvement, is describing this exact loop: find the leak, fix the logic behind it, make the fix explainable enough to survive the next reorg.

First 30 days

What the first month on a Cisco pricing desk looks like.

Week 1
Map the exceptions
Pull the last 90 days of discount exceptions and pricing requests by product line and region. Sit with Sales, Finance and the deal desk to hear where the current process creates friction, and where the same exception keeps getting re-approved.
Why first: the leak is usually visible in the data before anyone has named it out loud. Same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers program.
Week 2-3
Rank and pressure-test
Build a ranked view of the top margin opportunities against a real target, then pressure-test the top 3 with Sales and Finance before proposing a fix. Ship one repricing or process recommendation with a documented, auditable reason.
Why now: ranking against a hard target, then verifying the fix cross-functionally, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain stick.
Week 4
Make it repeatable
Turn the one-off fix into a template: a dashboard view, an approval reason format, and a checklist Sales and Finance both sign off on before the next exception request comes in.
Why this fast: creative solutions matter less if they only work once. Repeatability is the actual ask behind "process improvement."
