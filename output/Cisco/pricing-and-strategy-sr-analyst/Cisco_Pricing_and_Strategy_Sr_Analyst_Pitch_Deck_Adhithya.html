Why this deck exists

A portfolio this wide does not fail on one bad price. It fails on packaging that does not match how a segment actually buys.

What a portfolio like this runs into

Hardware, software and services sold under different licensing and consumption models mean the same discount KPI can mean something different in every segment, and an annual pricing review only catches it after the fact.
Packaging improvements get treated as a one-time project when they are really a recurring test: the bundle that worked last cycle drifts out of step with buyer behavior the next one.
The fix is a repeatable loop: read elasticity and the competitive signal, test a packaging or pricing-logic change on a narrow slice, then scale what holds.

What I have already run, at smaller scale

At Sensata, I ran the Bleeders and Leakers program: willingness-to-pay and competitive-gap data ranked against a hard revenue target, by region and product line.
I rebuilt the underlying pricing logic itself, which is why the 53% to 61% distributor gain held past the first repricing pass instead of drifting back.
I am now building PriceKeel, a subscription and consumption deal-pricing product, so I have tested packaging on both a hardware BU and a SaaS pricing model.
Support pricing and monetization strategy, analyze buyer behavior and willingness to pay, support annual pricing reviews and packaging improvements, build scalable pricing tools, monitor discount KPIs. That is the same loop I ran at Sensata, applied to a narrower portfolio than Cisco's.

First 30 days

What the first month on this pricing desk looks like.

Week 1
Read the buying-behavior signal
Pull the last pricing review's discount KPIs and competitive data by product line and licensing model. Sit with pricing and finance to find where willingness-to-pay data is thin or where a packaging bundle no longer matches how a segment buys.
Why first: the same move that opened the Bleeders and Leakers program: find where the data is quiet before proposing a fix.
Week 2-3
Test a packaging or pricing-logic change
Build or extend a scalable pricing model that ranks opportunities against the elasticity and competitive data, then test one packaging or pricing-logic change on a single product line before scaling it.
Why now: testing narrow before scaling is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain hold.
Week 4
Ship the KPI view
Turn the test into a discount and pricing KPI view the team monitors on a recurring cadence, with the packaging logic documented well enough to survive the next annual pricing review.
Why this fast: a pricing tool only earns its name if it gets monitored after the first win, not just built once.
