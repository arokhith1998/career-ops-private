Why this deck exists

A first dedicated deal desk hire is really four jobs stitched into one person: deal structuring, policy judgment, the Legal handoff, and the automation that keeps it all connected to billing.

What losing that discipline runs into

A first deal desk hire does not fail loudly. It fails as a quote approved on last quarter's discount policy, a redline that sits with Legal for a week because nobody gave it commercial context, or a signed deal that goes live with the wrong billing setup.
AEs, Legal, Customer Success and Finance all touch the same deal, so a gap in approval guidance or system handoff becomes everyone's guessing game at the next renewal, not just one person's backlog.
The fix is not another policy document. It is one person who treats quoting, contracting and the billing handoff as one connected system instead of three separate jobs.

What I have already run

At PriceKeel, I built the guardrail engine that scores discount exceptions against a company's own closed-won and closed-lost history, and the audit trail that gives Finance evidence for every approval, the same policy-and-exception judgment this seat runs on every deal.
At PriceKeel I also set up and run the CRM, CPQ, forecast reviews and deal approvals myself, so I have sat on the system-ownership side of the handoff, not only the approval side.
At Sensata, I shaped AP26 revenue models and multimillion-dollar customer quotes directly with senior executives and raised distributor margins from 53% to 61% by repricing the underlying contract terms, the same commercial-terms judgment a Legal redline runs on.
A Deal Desk Lead who owns daily operations, reviews pricing and discount exceptions, partners with Legal on redlines, and maintains the automation into billing is describing one connected loop, not four separate jobs. That is the loop I already run.

First 30 days

What the first month owning deal desk at Reducto looks like.

Week 1
Learn the automation and the contracting playbook
Sit with Account Executives, Legal and Operations to walk the existing deal desk automation, the order form, services agreement, DPA and BAA playbook, and shadow one deal from commercial terms through signature end to end.
Why first: the same instinct that had me learn PriceKeel's own CPQ configuration before changing anything on top of it.
Week 2-3
Own a deal desk cycle and one automation fix
Take end-to-end ownership of deal structure, discount review and approver routing for a live cohort of deals, and ship one integration or rule improvement that turns a recurring exception into a documented policy.
Why now: the same guardrail-to-rule instinct that built PriceKeel's discount-exception engine.
Week 4
Tighten the signature-to-billing handoff
Audit one signed deal's billing configuration, provisioning and contractual account settings against what the order form promised, and flag one renewal or amendment pattern worth a template update before the next cycle.
Why this fast: a quote-to-cash handoff only earns trust if it gets checked every cycle instead of assuming last quarter's setup still holds.
