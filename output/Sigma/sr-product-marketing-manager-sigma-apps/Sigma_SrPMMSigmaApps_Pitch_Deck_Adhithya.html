Why this deck exists

Write only for the IT reviewer and the business buyer tunes out. Write only for the business buyer and the IT review stalls the deal.

What losing that split costs a team like Sigma Apps

A no-code app platform sells on two different conversations in the same deal: can IT govern and scale it, and can the business team actually ship with it. Positioning that only answers one of those reads as a pitch deck, not a reason to buy.
Reps feel that gap first. A battlecard built for the IT conversation goes unused in the line-of-business meeting, and the field quietly writes its own version, off message and unmeasured.
The fix is not two sets of collateral. It is one narrative, built from what both buyers actually ask on calls, that a rep can use in either room without switching decks.

What I have already run, on a technical product

At Sensata, the new sensing-product launch had to read for OEM engineers who could spot a padded spec sheet and for the distributors who only cared whether the part sold, on the same launch.
I turned those specs into one positioning narrative, drove $2M+ in first-year revenue and a 15% conversion lift, then built the enablement (decks, case studies, competitive one-pagers) that made it usable in the field for 50+ reps.
That split is the same shape as IT leader versus line-of-business owner, just in a different industry.
Sigma Apps is asking for a PMM who understands both sides of the enterprise software conversation and writes one story for the field. I have run that exact split, at $2M+ scale.
