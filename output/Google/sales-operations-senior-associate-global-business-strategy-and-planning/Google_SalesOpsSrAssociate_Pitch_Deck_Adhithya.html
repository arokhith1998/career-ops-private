Why this deck exists

This req is one discipline wearing two hats: the model that survives scrutiny, and the judgment that turns it into one executive-ready call under a deadline.

What falls apart without that person

A central team that touches multiple areas of Google's Ads business across a range of topics cannot afford a slow translation: by the time a finding reaches an executive leader, the data behind it has to already be reconciled, not still being argued over.
Ad hoc corporate initiatives with global stakeholders do not come with a template. Without someone who can structure an ambiguous question into a prioritized scope fast, each one either stalls or arrives too late to shape the decision.
The fix is one person who treats the quantitative model and the executive narrative as one job, not a handoff between two people.

What I already run

At Safran, I led a 5-consultant team from an undefined market-entry question to a scoped SAM of 28 across 4 verticals, briefing the roadmap directly to their leadership on a fixed engagement clock.
At Sensata, I turned 1,200 scattered pricing opportunities into $433M in margin gaps leadership could prioritize, then carried that same rigor into AP26 revenue models directly with senior executives.
Both mean the ambiguous-to-structured loop this team runs daily is not new to me; I have already built the scope, the model and the room briefing in one motion.
Developing a prioritized scope for resolution in ambiguous situations, then executing the quantitative modeling behind it for executive leaders, describes this exact loop.

First 30 days

What the first month inside Strategy and Planning looks like.

Week 1
Map how a finding becomes an executive call
Sit with the team and the executive leaders it serves to learn how research results, business drivers and operating metrics currently move into a decision, and shadow one live workstream before touching anything.

Week 2-3
