It was not a one-time analysis. It had to survive a quarterly review cadence, which is the part of this kind of work that is easy to skip and expensive to skip.

The core business operations line in this posting, annual business planning, running business reviews and target setting, is close to the seat I already hold at Applied Materials. I align Sales, Engineering and Accounts into one view of the semiconductor market, then run the OKRs and the weekly and quarterly business reviews that carry P&L and revenue targets across multiple product groups. I have also hired and managed the teams behind those targets, at Plug Power and at Pixis, so the headcount side of business operations is not unfamiliar either. I carried that same discipline into AP26 revenue models and multimillion-dollar customer contracting directly with Sensata's senior executives, the kind of executive pushback and ambiguity this role describes.

My opinion on cross-functional strategy work is that it fails quietly, not loudly: a recommendation gets nodded through in one review, then nobody revisits it once the meeting ends. The fix is not a better deck. I also sit on the advertiser side this org serves: I carried P&L targets for an 8-client, $15K-$90K per client per month advertising portfolio at Pixis, running retail media and reporting straight to senior management, so the seller's view of a sales tools decision is not new to me. I automate the recurring half of my own analysis with AI agents built on Claude at Applied Materials today, and I would bring that same bias toward a defensible model and a working cadence to GBAI.
