Why this deck exists

A sales-tools strategy org is really two disciplines held by one person: the operational data that finds the insight, and the cadence that makes it stick for Director+ stakeholders.

What losing that discipline runs into

A strategy function that only produces a recommendation does not fail loudly. It fails as a plan nodded through in one review with a Director-level stakeholder, then quietly abandoned next quarter because no reporting cadence existed to check on it.
GBAI sits between global product leads, engineering, legal, compliance and finance, so a gap in process design or stakeholder alignment becomes everyone's guessing game at the next business review, not just one team's problem.
The fix is not another one-off analysis. It is someone who treats insight generation, reporting-framework design and executive alignment as one connected discipline.

What I have already run

At Applied Materials, I align Sales, Engineering and Accounts into one strategic view, then own the business operations cadence, OKRs, annual target setting, weekly and quarterly business reviews, across multiple product groups.
At Sensata, I designed the Bleeders and Leakers reporting framework that made 1,200 pricing opportunities actionable, then carried that same discipline into AP26 revenue models and contracting directly with senior executives.
Both mean I have sat on the insight-generation side and the stakeholder-alignment side of this exact loop, not just one.
A Strategy and Operations Lead who drives complex cross-functional initiatives end to end, designs reporting frameworks, and builds a network across Director-level stakeholders is describing this exact loop: structure the ambiguity, build the process, keep every function aligned behind it.

Why this fast: a cross-functional initiative only earns its keep if a cadence exists to re-check it every cycle instead of trusting the first readout.
