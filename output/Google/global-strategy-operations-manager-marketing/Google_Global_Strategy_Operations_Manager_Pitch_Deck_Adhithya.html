Why this deck exists

An annual plan is really two disciplines held by one seat: the analysis that sets the investment, and the cadence that keeps it honest all year.

What an annual plan runs into without that seat

Marketing, Product, Finance and Engineering each hold a piece of the same plan. Without one owner connecting them, the plan gets agreed in a kickoff room and then drifts, because no one function alone can keep four stakeholders aligned to it quarter over quarter.
Performance data piles up, but turning it into a recommendation that actually changes the approach, not just a retrospective slide, takes someone who can hold the quantitative case and the executive relationship at once.
The fix is one person who treats investment analysis, cross-functional alignment and executive communication as a single discipline instead of three separate jobs.

What I already run

At Applied Materials, I align Sales, Engineering and Accounts into one strategic view, then run the OKRs and quarterly reviews that keep that view funding the right priorities all year.
At Sensata, I built the Bleeders and Leakers system that made 1,200 opportunities actionable, then carried that analysis directly into AP26 revenue models with senior executives.
Both mean I have built the model and sat in the room where it gets funded, not just one side of that work.
Partnering with leadership to set the annual plan, working across Marketing, Product, Finance and Engineering, and turning performance data into a recommendation that adjusts the approach describes this exact loop: build the case, align the stakeholders, keep proving it with data.

What the first month inside Global Marketing Strategy and Operations looks like.

Week 1
Map the annual plan and its owners
Sit with Marketing, Product, Finance and Engineering stakeholders to learn how this year's plan was built and funded, and shadow one existing performance review end to end before changing anything.

Week 2-3
Own one performance evaluation end to end
