Why this deck exists

A global strategy and operations seat is really two disciplines held by one person: the data that finds the opportunity, and the regional framework that keeps everyone accountable to it.

What losing that discipline runs into

A global program that only ships one rolled-up number does not fail loudly. It fails as a plan every region nods through in one review, then quietly reinterprets locally because no region-level scorecard exists to check progress against.
A business spanning the Americas, EMEA and APAC turns that gap into three different versions of the same initiative within two quarters, each defensible on its own and none comparable to the others.
The fix is not a better global deck. It is a framework that gives every region its own accountability line from day one, so the rollup is a summary, not the only source of truth.

What I have already run

At Sensata, I designed the Bleeders and Leakers framework across 3 regions, 4 product families and 20 product lines, giving each region its own scorecard by salesperson and product line instead of one combined number.
I then carried that same discipline into AP26 revenue models and multimillion-dollar contracting directly with senior executives, and into a 10+ competitor benchmark that became an executed GTM recommendation.
At Applied Materials, I run the business review cadence, OKRs and quarterly reviews, behind P&L targets across multiple product groups, so the framework stays current past launch.
A manager who owns global go-to-market strategy end to end, builds frontline partner planning and accountability frameworks across regions, and takes ambiguous initiatives from sizing through executive alignment to launch and measurement, is describing this exact loop: structure the ambiguity, build the regional framework, keep every region accountable to it.
