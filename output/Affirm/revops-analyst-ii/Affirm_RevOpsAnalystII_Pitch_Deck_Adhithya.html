Why this deck exists

The risk at the top of the funnel is not too few leads. It is routing rules and tooling that quietly drift as volume grows.

What a growing lead-to-account motion runs into

Lead routing logic, scoring thresholds and data enrichment rules get built once, then nobody owns keeping them current as new sources, campaigns and account types get added.
Marketing automation platforms and Salesforce drift apart quietly: a field renamed on one side stops mattering on the other, and nobody notices until a rep asks why their queue is empty.
The fix is not another dashboard review. It is one person who owns the routing rules, automates the handoff, and documents the change so the next person does not reverse-engineer it.

What I have already run, hands on

At Sensata, I built the SQL and GA4 scoring layer that told the team which of 50,000+ connected devices were showing real buying intent, not just traffic.

Lead routing, lead lifecycle management, marketing automation and Salesforce administration are describing this exact loop: own the rule, automate the handoff, document the change, and keep it that way as volume scales.

First 30 days

What the first month on Affirm's Revenue Operations team looks like.

Week 1
Trace the current lead flow
Map today's lead routing logic, scoring rules and data enrichment sources end to end, from first form fill or partner referral through Salesforce assignment, and flag where the rule and the reality have drifted apart.
Why first: the drift is usually visible in the queue data before anyone names it. Same move that started the WordPress and Pardot rebuild at Plug Power.
Week 2-3
Ship the routing fix
Rebuild the specific routing rule or workflow automation causing the worst drift, document the change, and pressure-test it against real leads with Sales and Business Systems before it goes live.
Why now: the same sequence, fix then document, cut cost per lead 50% at Plug Power and made the change stick past the first quarter.
Week 4
Train the team on it
Turn the fix into a short process-change training for the revenue teams that touch it, and set up the attribution view that shows whether the new routing is actually converting better.
Why this fast: a routing fix nobody is trained on reverts to the old habit within a month. Training is the step that makes it permanent.
