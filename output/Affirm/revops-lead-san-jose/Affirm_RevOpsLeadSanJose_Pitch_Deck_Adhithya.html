Why this deck exists

The risk is not slower deal flow. It is territory rules and stage definitions that stop agreeing across Sales, ISV, Partnerships and Marketplace.

What a multi-motion mid-funnel runs into

Sales, ISV, Strategic Partnerships and Marketplace each own accounts and stages a little differently, and an account-to-rep mapping fix in one motion can quietly break an ownership assumption another team is relying on.
Without one shared segmentation and stage-definition standard, territory hygiene and forecasting accuracy drift apart exactly as deal volume across the merchant motions grows.
The fix is not another handoff meeting. It is a documented territory and process standard, plus a change-management routing habit that keeps Salesforce and BI data in sync as coverage scales.

What I have already run, at smaller scale

At Sensata, I built the Bleeders and Leakers Power BI dashboard: 1,200 opportunities segmented by region and product line against a $627M target, with one consistent stage and win-rate definition shared by every team.
I did not stop at the dashboard. I redesigned the deal structures underneath the worst gaps, which is why the 53% to 61% distributor margin lift held past the first quarter.
I run that same loop today at PriceKeel: CRM and CPQ, opportunity management standards, and a documentation trail Finance can point to, because territory governance only works if someone owns it after launch.
Territory planning and governance, pipeline health and forecasting hygiene, and cross-functional governance across ISV, Strategic Partnerships and Marketplace are describing this exact loop: fix the segmentation once, document the ownership rule, keep it from drifting as coverage scales.

First 30 days

What the first month on Affirm's Revenue Operations team looks like.

Week 1
Map where ownership disagrees
Pull stage definitions, territory rules and account-to-rep mapping as they exist across Sales, ISV, Strategic Partnerships and Marketplace today, and trace where two motions are using the same term or the same account boundary to mean different things.
Why first: the disagreement is usually visible in the data before anyone names it. Same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers program.
Week 2-3
Ship the segmentation standard
Document one territory and stage-definition standard, build the account-to-rep mapping and ownership-transition rule behind it, and pressure-test it with Sales, ISV and Partnerships against real accounts before it goes into Salesforce.
Why now: ranking against one real target, then verifying with the cross-functional group, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain stick.
Week 4
Standardize the governance
Turn the Week 1 findings into a documentation and change-management standard for territory and mid-funnel process updates, and partner with Revenue Enablement to train Sales, ISV and Partnerships on the new rules and tooling.
Why this fast: a segmentation model matters less if the ownership rules behind it stay ad hoc. Repeatability is the actual ask behind "territory hygiene."
