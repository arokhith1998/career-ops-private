Why this deck exists

The risk in merchant financing pricing is not a bad feature. It is a feature nobody can prove worked, or defend to Finance six months later.

What a credit and pricing roadmap runs into

Credit, pricing, commercial, design and engineering all touch the same merchant financing offering, and a pricing change that looks right in isolation can quietly break a credit assumption another team is relying on.
Without a shared way to segment impact and document the decision, "we shipped a new pricing feature" and "we know it worked" end up being two different claims, and only one of them is provable later.
The fix is not another dashboard nobody trusts. It is a segmentation model tied to a real target, plus an audit trail that lets Finance and Credit sign off on the decision before and after it ships.

What I have already run, at smaller scale

At Sensata, I built the Bleeders and Leakers Power BI dashboard: 1,200 pricing opportunities segmented by region and product line against a $627M target, with one shared definition of what a "gap" meant.
I did not stop at the dashboard. I redesigned the deal structures underneath the worst gaps, which is why the 53% to 61% distributor margin lift held past the first quarter.
I run that same loop today at PriceKeel: a guardrail engine that reads a live discount exception and an audit trail Finance can point to, because a pricing feature only counts once someone can prove it worked.
Defining product strategy, creating new features for merchant financing, analyzing feature impact and partnering across credit, pricing, commercial, design and engineering are all describing this exact loop: ship the feature, measure it against a real target, and keep the proof.

First 30 days

What the first month on Affirm's Credit & Pricing team looks like.

Week 1
Map the merchant financing pricing surface
Pull the current pricing and credit features across the merchant portfolio and the metrics each one is measured against today, then find where credit, pricing and commercial teams are using the same term to mean different things.
Why first: the disagreement is usually visible in the data before anyone names it. Same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers program.
Week 2-3
Ship the first measured feature
Pick the merchant financing feature with the clearest impact case, define the target and the segmentation up front, and ship it with design and engineering with the measurement plan built in rather than bolted on after launch.
Why now: ranking against one real target before shipping, then verifying after, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain stick.
Week 4
Document the decision trail
Turn the Week 1 findings and the Week 2-3 result into a standard for how credit, pricing and commercial sign off on a feature before and after it ships, and share it back with the cross-functional group.
Why this fast: a measured result matters less if nobody can find the reasoning behind it later. Repeatability is the actual ask behind "analyzing feature impact."
