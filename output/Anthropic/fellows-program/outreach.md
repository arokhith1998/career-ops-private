## 1) Research proposal (the lead artifact - paste into the application's open field)

> **Research Question:** How does LLM-enabled price-discovery shift pricing dynamics in low-liquidity industrial markets?
>
> **Why this question.** I led global pricing for Sensata's Industrial BU - a market where price formation is opaque, customer-specific, and historically driven by relationship rather than transparent benchmarks. As LLMs enter industrial procurement (buyers using ChatGPT and Perplexity to research vendor pricing, sellers using Claude to build pricing rationale), the floor and ceiling of negotiable price ranges are starting to compress in observable ways. This is an empirically tractable Economics & Policy question with a clear social-welfare framing: does AI-enabled price-discovery transfer surplus from sellers to buyers, or does it merely reorganize the information asymmetry?
>
> **What I'd build over 4 months.**
> 1. A longitudinal dataset of industrial-product price quotes pre- and post-LLM adoption, drawn from public RFP datasets, federal procurement portals (SAM.gov, USAspending), and historical sensor + components catalogs.
> 2. A quasi-experimental design comparing markets with high vs low LLM adoption among buyers (proxied via firm-level Anthropic + OpenAI customer-stack signals and industry-segment LLM-tooling penetration).
> 3. A Claude-based agentic simulation of buyer-seller pricing negotiation under different information asymmetry conditions, used to triangulate the empirical findings against a theoretical baseline.
>
> **Why I'm the person to do it.** I sit at the unusual intersection of industrial-pricing practitioner and LLM builder. Four Anthropic Claude certifications, two shipped LLM products (SwipeHire on OpenAI API, NSE Bot on Claude API with agentic reasoning over news + catalyst data), and a year leading pricing in the exact market category I would study. The discipline of the empirical practitioner is the research advantage here, not a substitute for academic ML credentials.
>
> **What the public output would look like.** A working-paper-style submission on arXiv or SSRN suitable for NBER's Economics of AI workstream or AEA P&P. Title shape: "AI-Enabled Price-Discovery in Low-Liquidity Industrial Markets: A Quasi-Experimental Evidence Base." Co-authored with the Economics & Policy mentor.

*Word count: ~360. Tighten or expand to fit the application's character cap.*
