Why this deck exists

A pricing and packaging recommendation that skips the KPI loop does not fail loudly. It fails as a tier nobody can defend at the next review, quarters after it shipped.

What losing that discipline runs into

Customer research and willingness-to-pay data are easy to collect and easy to misread. Without a defined set of metrics and a model that projects second-order effects, a packaging change looks right in the room and wrong two quarters later.
Product, Sales, Marketing and Operations all touch the same pricing decision, so a gap in project management or in the business case becomes everyone's guessing game at the next cross-functional review.
The fix is not another workshop. It is someone who treats research, modeling and governance as one connected discipline instead of three separate jobs.

What I have already run

At Sensata, I converted customer research and competitive data into pricing recommendations, then built the Power BI system that defined the KPIs proving whether they worked across 1,200 opportunities.
At PriceKeel, I built the audit trail and durable governance process that keeps a pricing decision defensible after the fact, not just at the moment it is proposed.
Both mean I have sat on the research-and-recommendation side and the KPI-and-governance side of the same decision, not just one or the other.
A Pricing and Packaging Manager who designs analytical tests, defines effectiveness metrics, models revenue impact and runs cross-functional execution is describing this exact loop: research the need, model the decision, prove it held.

First 30 days

What the first month owning pricing and packaging recommendations at Bluebeam looks like.

Week 1
Learn the current pricing and packaging stack
Sit with Product, Sales and Marketing to walk the Build and Construct Division's current tier structure, pricing data sources and decision-approval process, and shadow one cross-functional pricing review end to end.
Why first: the same instinct that had me learn PriceKeel's own CRM and CPQ configuration before building anything on top of it.
Week 2-3
Run one willingness-to-pay test and its model
Take ownership of one customer research or analytical test end to end: define the metrics it needs to prove, build the revenue-impact model with second-order effects, and validate it against the last two quarters of historical data.
Why now: the same validate-before-ship discipline that made the Bleeders and Leakers dashboard trustworthy in a leadership review.
Week 4
Ship a data-driven business case, cross-functionally
Turn the test into a business case, present it to Product, Sales, Marketing and Operations, build alignment on the recommendation, and document the KPI and governance trail for the next cycle.
Why this fast: a pricing recommendation only earns its keep if the cross-functional group that has to live with it signs off early.
