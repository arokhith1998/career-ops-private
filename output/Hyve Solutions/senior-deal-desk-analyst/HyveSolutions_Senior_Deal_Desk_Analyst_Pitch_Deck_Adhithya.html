Why this deck exists

The risk in a complex RFQ is not the first quote. It is the cost gap nobody catches until after the margin is already committed.

What a high-volume RFQ/RFP desk runs into

Materials, labor, logistics, duties, tooling and warranty inputs come from six different functions, so an unsupported assumption in any one of them can sit in a quote until Finance catches it after the deal is priced.
The analyst owning approvals has no direct authority over Sales, Engineering or Supply Chain, so influence has to come from the quality of the model and the evidence behind it, not a title.
A one-off pricing file does not scale. The fix is a standing approval record and decision history a reviewer can trust without re-deriving it.

What I have already run, hands on

At Sensata, I ran deal-level margin governance across customer quotes and distributor contracts, recovering $700K+ in two quarters by closing the gaps a Bleeders and Leakers review surfaced.
At PriceKeel, I designed the guardrail engine and audit trail that documents every pricing exception with evidence Finance can review, not a verbal justification.
At Applied Materials, I automate the recurring half of that analysis with AI agents built on Claude, so a refresh is a review, not a rebuild.
Lead end-to-end RFQ/RFP pricing, own governance and approvals, find the cost gaps and drive resolution across functions without direct authority: this is the exact loop I already run.
