Why this deck exists

The hard part of a Deal Desk is not finding the data. It is reviewing every discount exception fast enough that Sales does not stall, while still protecting gross margin.

What balancing revenue and margin at scale runs into

Deals arrive in every size at once: multi-million-dollar global accounts and small/midsize deals that still need a fast answer, so the review speed cannot change with deal size.
Discount approval, competitive-dynamics analysis and sales-incentive tracking sit with different teams, Sales, Sales Ops, Finance and Product, so a process only scales if all of them trust the same numbers.
Revenue and margin goals pull in opposite directions across products, channels, segments and geographies, so a one-size discount rule either gives away margin or loses winnable deals.

What I have already run, building this exact system

At PriceKeel, I run a guardrail engine that reviews every discount exception deal-by-deal against competitive dynamics, the same discount-approval mandate this team owns.
I designed the audit trail that gives Finance evidence for every discount decision, selling it against ad hoc approvals, the same cross-functional trust a deal desk needs from Sales, Finance and Product.
At Sensata, I balanced revenue against margin across 3 regions, 4 product families and 20 product lines, the same multi-segment tradeoff this role runs at Verkada's scale.
A Senior Associate who reviews discount approvals deal-by-deal, balances revenue against margin across products, channels, segments and geographies, and builds a process that scales with deal volume, is describing one connected loop. I have run that loop twice: once inside a company's pricing organization, once as the founder who had to build the tooling from scratch.

First 30 days

What the first month in Pricing & Commercial Operations at Verkada looks like.

Week 1
Map the deal and discount surface
Pull last quarter's discount-approval requests, deal sizes and win/loss by product, channel, segment and geography. Sit with Sales, Sales Ops, Finance and Product to hear where the current approval process slows a deal down.
Why first: the leak is usually visible in the data before anyone has named it out loud. Same move that started the Bleeders and Leakers program.
Week 2-3
Build and pressure-test the guardrail
Set discount-approval thresholds informed by competitive dynamics and win-rate data, test them against a sample of real deals ranging from multi-million-dollar accounts to small/midsize ones, then document the rule so Finance can audit it later.
Why now: modeling against a hard target, then verifying the fix cross-functionally, is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain stick instead of drifting back.
Week 4
Ship the first revenue/margin project
Pick one independent opportunity the data surfaced, a sales-incentive gap or a channel pricing leak, build the business case and start scaling it, the same self-directed move that grew into the Bleeders and Leakers program.
Why this fast: a deal desk earns trust the moment it finds money nobody asked it to find.
