Why this deck exists

A product that already works has a harder roadmap problem than a greenfield one: every backlog item has to earn its complexity against the simplicity that won customers in the first place. That is a sequencing discipline, not a shipping-speed one.

What that actually requires

Deciding what ships first across a known, sprawling backlog (integrations, templates, credit memos, promos) with no blank-slate excuse.
Measuring the bet in active users, feature adoption and retention, not shipped-feature count.
Keeping Engineering, Design, Data Science, Product Marketing, Support, Sales and Risk aligned behind one sequencing call.

What I have already built at that layer

Owned a 100+-feature backlog for Sensata's sensor roadmap with Engineering across 3 regions, cutting time-to-market 20% by deciding what mattered most each sprint.
Built KPI frameworks in SQL and GA4 across 50K+ connected devices to track the adoption and retention signals a feature actually needs to clear.
Equipped reps with sales enablement and aligned Engineering, Sales and regional stakeholders behind one roadmap, lifting win rates 30%.
The through-line: sequencing a sprawling, already-live backlog against adoption and retention math, not shipping as many features as possible, is the same discipline Sensata's roadmap ran on.

A first pass at sequencing the backlog

Accounting integrations, credit memos and promos all earn their place. Which one earns it first is a product-judgment call, not a coin flip.

Accounting Integrations
The widest segment unlock
Technology and Professional Services customers likely already live inside QuickBooks, Xero or NetSuite; closing that loop removes a manual reconciliation step for the segment with the highest switching cost once it is automated away.
Why first: the same segment-level adoption instinct that built KPI frameworks across 50K+ devices at Sensata, before feature count.
Credits & Credit Memos
The detail that protects trust, not the one that grows usage
Invoices are money, and a credit memo is the moment a mistake becomes a customer-trust problem. It is the retention lever in the role's own growth metric, not the active-user one.
Why next: the same close-attention discipline that carried multimillion-dollar customer quotes at Sensata without a pricing error reaching a customer.
Promos & Coupons
Sequence last, not never
A promo or coupon changes how a payer interacts with the invoice itself, the riskiest surface to touch before the dashboard and builder are already earning trust on the basics.
Why last: the same simplicity discipline that kept Sensata's 100+-feature backlog shipping 25% faster, a feature that is not load-bearing does not jump the queue.
