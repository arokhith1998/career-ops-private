A first pass at the battlecard gap

Fintech challengers and traditional banks are not the same objection. One battlecard for both loses the call either way.

Against Fintech Challengers
Win on trust and support, not features
A fast-moving fintech competitor usually matches on features within a quarter. The durable wedge is operational trust: support response time, uptime history and how agentic payment features are explained, not just that they exist.
Why this framing: the same instinct behind turning technical specs into buyer-facing positioning at Sensata, where the spec never closed a deal on its own.
Against Traditional Banks
Win on speed and clarity, not scale
A traditional bank wins on scale and regulatory comfort; it loses on speed of setup, clarity of pricing and how fast support actually responds. The battlecard needs reps ready for a founder who already has a bank account and is asking why switch.
Why this framing: the same competitive-benchmarking discipline that shaped a 12% YoY revenue gain at Sensata, built on scoring 10+ named competitors, not a generic feature grid.
Where AI Shortens the Loop
Automate the monitoring, not the judgment
Competitor pricing pages, release notes and support-review threads change weekly. An AI agent can watch all three and flag what actually shifted the argument, so the battlecard updates in days, not at the next quarterly refresh.
Why this framing: the same Claude-agent habit already running at Applied Materials, pointed at a sharper, faster-moving target.

First 30 days

What the first month on the banking and payments launch calendar would look like.

Week 1
Sit with the next launch's actual customers
Talk to founders and finance teams already waiting on the next feature before writing a single line of positioning, the same customer-interview discipline that shaped Sensata's launches.
Why first: a positioning draft written before the customer conversation is a guess dressed up as strategy.
Week 2-3
Build the launch plan and the battlecard together
Write positioning, the campaign brief for Brand and Creative, and the Revenue battlecard as one package, not three handoffs, the same cross-functional sequencing that ran Sensata's $2M+ launch.
Why now: a battlecard built after the launch ships arrives too late for the first wave of deals.
Week 4
Ship, train Revenue, and measure against adoption
Launch, run the enablement session with Revenue, and set the measurement plan against adoption and business impact, the same discipline that turned Sensata's launches into 15% conversion lifts and 30% higher win rates.
Why this fast: a launch without a measurement plan set on day one gets judged by opinion instead of data.
