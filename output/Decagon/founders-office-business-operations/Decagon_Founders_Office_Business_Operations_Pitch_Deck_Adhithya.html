Why this deck exists

Enterprise GTM, pricing strategy, company OKRs and internal tooling are four jobs that fail apart and work as one connected loop.

What breaks when they stay separate

A pricing strategy revamp with no tool behind it becomes a slide deck nobody updates past the first quarter.
OKRs set without touching the GTM motion they are supposed to measure become a reporting exercise, not a steering wheel.
Internal tooling built without the strategy behind it becomes a dashboard nobody trusts enough to act on.

What I have already run as one loop

At Sensata, I built the pricing strategy, the tool that tracked it, and the quarterly review that used it, as one piece of work.
At Applied Materials, I turn Sales, Engineering and Accounts input into one GTM view, then run the OKRs that measure it.
At PriceKeel, the company I founded, I built the strategy, the CRM/CPQ system, and the deal-approval process myself, with nobody to hand any piece off to.
A Founder's Office seat whose scope shifts between GTM, pricing, OKRs and tooling based on company needs is describing a generalist who treats all four as one loop, not four separate specialists in a trench coat.

First 30 days

What the first month inside Decagon's Founder's Office could look like.

Week 1
Map the current GTM, pricing and OKR loop
Sit with the Founder's Office and the GTM team to trace how the current pricing strategy, enterprise GTM motion and company OKRs connect today, and where one of the three is flying blind on the other two.
Why first: the same mapping instinct that had me trace PriceKeel's own deal flow before building the guardrail engine on top of it.
Week 2-3
Ship one piece of internal tooling against a real gap
Pick the one disconnected piece from Week 1, most likely pricing strategy without a tool behind it, and build the internal tool or process that closes it, the way the Bleeders and Leakers dashboard closed that same gap at Sensata.
Why now: a tool proves itself by getting used in the next business review, not by living in a deck.
Week 4
Run it through one full OKR review
Put the new tool or process in front of the next OKR review, so the loop from strategy to tool to cadence is proven end to end, not just assembled.
Why this fast: a process only earns its keep once it survives a real review, run by someone other than the person who built it.
