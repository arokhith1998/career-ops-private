Why this deck exists

A Founder's Office seat carries two different jobs at once: run today's fire drill, and build the system so it is not a fire drill next quarter.

What the seat actually demands

A founder-level initiative shows up as a sentence, not a brief. Someone has to turn it into goals, milestones and an owner before any team can move.
Functions that are still founder-led or ad-hoc need the same rigour a central Centre of Excellence would bring: a playbook, a dashboard and a cadence that holds after the builder leaves the room.
AI transformation is the third leg: finding the workflow burning the most hours and rebuilding it AI-first before asking a team to scale it.

What I have already run, on all three

First 30 days

What the first month as Pepper Content's Senior Manager, Founder's Office looks like.

Week 1
Map what is unowned
Sit with the Founder and the central COE team to find which initiatives, business units and workflows are still ad-hoc, and shadow one US-facing review end to end.

Week 2-3
Build the operating layer
Turn the highest-leverage workflow into a definition, a dashboard and a review cadence the Founder can see within 24 hours, and partner with product and engineering to rebuild the manual steps AI-first.
Why now: building the model with the real stakeholders in the room is what made the 53% to 61% distributor gain stick.
Week 4
Ship v1 and hand off the first playbook
Launch a working v1 rather than wait for a perfect plan, measure it against speed, cost or quality, then document it as the first playbook the COE can scale to a second business unit.
Why this fast: a function only counts as fixed once it can run without you in the room, and you have moved to the next problem.
