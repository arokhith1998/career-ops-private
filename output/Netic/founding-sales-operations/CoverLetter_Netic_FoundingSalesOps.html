Your posting says there is no territory model, reporting stack or rulebook to inherit, and that is the exact build I keep signing up for. As founder of PriceKeel, I am standing up our CRM, CPQ configuration and deal-approval workflow with no playbook handed down: every rule starts as a dispute, gets resolved against the data, and becomes the precedent the next deal runs on.

Before PriceKeel I ran the closest thing Sensata had to a territory and capacity model: the FY2026 Bleeders and Leakers Power BI build, 1,200 opportunities tracked against a $627M target across 3 regions, 4 product families and 20 product lines, with win rate and stage distribution broken out by salesperson. I recovered $700K+ in margin in two quarters by resolving pricing disputes from the data instead of precedent, and raised distributor margins from 53% to 61% by making the same kind of channel-conflict call a Rules of Engagement owner has to make and document.

My honest opinion on a first sales ops build: the metrics layer only earns trust once the dispute process behind it is boring and repeatable, so I would start with Rules of Engagement before the dashboard, not after. At Applied Materials I already automate the repetitive side of this with AI agents built on Claude, and at Plug Power I automated the Salesforce, Adobe Analytics and Google Analytics stack for closed-loop reporting, so admin-level Salesforce work is not new territory for me.
