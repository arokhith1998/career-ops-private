Why this deck exists

A first performance hire cannot just run ads. They have to build the system that makes the ads accountable.

What a first-hire mandate runs into

With no existing paid function, the first 90 days are not about one good campaign. They are about a reporting and testing infrastructure that holds up once budget scales into seven figures.
Without audience pipelines, a creative-testing cadence and bid-management rules built into the stack from day one, every new channel added becomes a one-off instead of a repeatable system.
The fix is not more spend. It is treating paid media as an engineering problem: pipelines, dashboards and bid rules that keep working after the person who built them moves to the next channel.

What I have already built, at smaller scale

At Plug Power, I owned $100K/month paid budgets across Google, Microsoft, Meta and LinkedIn alone, and built the automated reporting (WordPress, Pardot, Salesforce, Adobe Analytics, GTM) that made every dollar traceable to pipeline stage.
At Pixis, I ran paid social, mobile UA and retail media as one P&L for an 8-client, 8-person-team portfolio, with a standing testing cadence behind the 3x ROAS result.
At Applied Materials, I automate recurring reporting today with agentic AI workflows built on Claude, the same AI-tool fluency a first paid hire needs to document from day one.
A first dedicated performance hire with real budget from day one is being asked to build the machine, not just buy the media. That is the exact gap between a channel operator and someone who has already owned the budget, the reporting and the testing loop alone.
