## A) Role summary

Anthropic's Fellows Program is a 4-month full-time empirical research fellowship structured around 5 workstreams: AI Safety, AI Security, ML Systems & Performance, Reinforcement Learning, and Economics & Policy. Fellows work under direct mentorship from senior Anthropic researchers, produce a public research output (typically a paper), and work in Berkeley, London, or remote-US/UK/Canada. Stipend $3,850/wk + benefits + ~$15K/mo compute funding for the 4-month duration. Acceptance into the program does not guarantee a full-time offer; some fellows transition to full-time roles, but those would require separate sponsorship which Anthropic explicitly does not provide for fellows.

>
> **Why this question:** I led global pricing for Sensata's Industrial BU - a market where price formation is opaque, customer-specific, and historically driven by relationship rather than transparent benchmarks. As LLMs enter industrial procurement (buyers now using ChatGPT and Perplexity to research vendor pricing, sellers using Claude to build pricing rationale), the floor and ceiling of negotiable price ranges are starting to compress in observable ways. This is an empirically tractable Economics & Policy question with a clear social-welfare framing: does AI-enabled price-discovery transfer surplus from sellers to buyers, or does it merely reorganize the information asymmetry?

> **What I'd build:** (a) a longitudinal dataset of industrial-product price quotes pre- and post-LLM adoption, drawn from public RFP datasets + procurement portals; (b) a quasi-experimental design comparing markets with high vs low LLM adoption among buyers; (c) a Claude-based agentic simulation of buyer-seller pricing negotiation under different information asymmetry conditions, used to triangulate the empirical findings.
>
> **Why I'm the person:** I sit at the unusual intersection of industrial-pricing-practitioner and LLM-builder. I have 4 Anthropic Claude certifications, 2 shipped LLM products, and a year leading pricing in the exact market category I'd study.

This is the strongest play. The proposal earns the application because it's specific, the user is uniquely positioned to write it, and it's narrowly Economics & Policy-shaped.
