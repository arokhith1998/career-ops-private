## Full JD text

Why Socure? Socure is building the identity trust infrastructure for the digital economy, verifying 100% of good identities in real time and stopping fraud before it starts. The mission is big, the problems are complex, and the impact is felt by businesses, governments, and millions of people every day.

We hire people who want that level of responsibility. People who move fast, think critically, act like owners, and care deeply about solving customer problems with precision.

About the role: We are looking for a Senior Product Manager to build and take to market a new category of products at Socure: proactive identity notifications. These are subscription products that continuously monitor identities on our customers' behalf and push an alert when something material about that identity changes.

This is a zero-to-one role with a near-term launch. You will own the first product in this line end to end: defining what good looks like, closing any performance gaps that stand between us and a product customers will pay for, shaping the event contract and subscription model, and running it from beta through GA.

Data is the heart of this role. You will establish the performance metrics that define success, decide what we need to source and what a candidate source has to prove, and make the call on which sources have earned their place. A dedicated Data Partnerships team runs discovery, negotiation, licensing, and vendor management against the brief you set.

You will also own commercialization: pricing and packaging, commercial terms, early-adopter recruitment, beta and GA phasing, enablement, and coordinating every function to a launch date.

What you'll do:

Product definition and launch
- Own the roadmap, definition, and launch of proactive identity notification products, from first beta customer through general availability.
- Define the customer-facing event contract: what the notification contains, its lifecycle semantics, and how customers consume and act on it.
- Design a systematic customer feedback loop so every notification we send makes the next one better.
- Begin the product definition for the next event types on the roadmap.

Data source strategy
- Own what we source and why: define the gaps in our coverage, specify what a candidate source must prove, and set the bar it has to clear.
- Run rigorous, hypothesis-driven evaluations that measure incremental lift rather than headline coverage claims, and make the buy, build, or walk-away call.
- Specify the licensing, retention, and derivative-use terms the product needs.
- Hold the portfolio to its KPIs.

Performance measurement and KPIs
- Define and instrument the KPIs for this product line, including coverage, precision, and recency, and build internal benchmarking capability.
- Define the target for each KPI that constitutes winning in the market.
- Quantify the incremental value a subscriber receives per period, and use it to inform pricing and positioning.
- Use SQL and analytics tooling to interrogate the data yourself rather than waiting on someone else's analysis.

Architecture and platform partnership
- Partner with platform engineering teams to define how identity signals are resolved, merged across sources, and published, and make the architectural tradeoff calls.
- Shape a generalized, reusable notification mechanism so each new event type is faster to ship than the last.
- Balance latency, cost, and refresh cadence against the customer value each incremental improvement buys.

Commercialization and go-to-market
- Define pricing, packaging, and commercial terms for a subscription product, in partnership with Legal, Finance, and Revenue.
- Identify, recruit, and work hand-in-hand with early-adopter and design-partner customers.
- Establish and track beta and GA phases, and coordinate cross-functional readiness to a committed date.
- Build the positioning, marketing plan, and sales enablement materials with Product Marketing.

Customer and competitive research
- Build a firm, evidence-based read on how we perform against market leaders and where our real differentiation lies.
- Understand the bar customers will actually accept, and be willing to say which customers and use cases we should not sell to yet.
- Assess and design around the business risk of getting a high-stakes identity signal wrong.

What you bring:
- Experience: 5+ years in product management, preferably in identity verification, fraud prevention, data products, or other data- and ML-driven products.
- Data product instinct: Demonstrated experience evaluating, acquiring, and operationalizing acquired data.
- Technical expertise: Strong understanding of APIs, SQL, databases, event-driven architectures, and product architecture generally.
- Analytical skills: Comfortable working with data directly, writing queries, and defining measurement methodology.
- Commercial judgment on data deals: Has been the product voice in data or technology vendor negotiations.
- Commercial ownership: Experience taking a product to market, covering pricing, packaging, beta programs, and launch.
- Product judgment: Ability to balance technical complexity, customer needs, regulatory constraints, and business impact.
- Customer focus: Experience working directly with customers, especially in complex or high-stakes environments.
- Communication: Strong ability to explain complex technical and data concepts clearly to both technical and non-technical audiences.
- Collaboration: Proven ability to work cross-functionally with Engineering, Data Science, Legal, and go-to-market teams.

Preferred: Familiarity with consumer data regulatory frameworks such as GLBA and FCRA; experience with entity resolution, identity graphs, or record-linkage systems; experience building a subscription or usage-based data product from zero to first revenue.
