## Full JD Text

About PostHog

PostHog is your product's context layer. It ingests and stores your analytics, errors, replays, and business data so you and your agents can query and act on it.

We launched out of Y Combinator's W20 cohort. We've since shipped more than a dozen products, including: a suite of analytics and observability tools (product analytics, replay vision, AI observability, error tracking); PostHog MCP for users and their agents to query and act on their product and business data; a Data warehouse so users can query product and customer data together using custom SQL insights.

We are: Product-led (more than 500K organizations have installed PostHog, mostly driven by word-of-mouth); Default alive (revenue is growing incredibly quickly, and we're very efficient); Well-funded (we've raised more than $180m from some of the world's top investors).

Things we care about: Transparency (public company handbook, shared revenue and board notes internally); Autonomy (engineers lead product teams and make product decisions); Shipping fast (small, highly-efficient teams who own products end-to-end); Time for building (natively remote, async-first, meeting-free Tuesdays and Thursdays); Ambition; Being weird.

Product at PostHog

Product management plays a slightly different role at PostHog than at most other companies and is incredibly important. Instead of micro-managing engineers, PMs are responsible for research, data, and setting high-level context across the organization. They work across multiple products to determine how products are being used, what the competitive landscape is like, and how users are feeling about PostHog. PMs at PostHog are very discovery-focused and don't engage in delivery almost at all.

Among other things, they: run growth reviews for products that have product-market-fit; dive into data research projects to answer challenging questions without obvious answers; organize user interviews; provide just-in-time product feedback for new and existing features, following principles of shipping and iterating quickly; coach product engineers on "how to do product."

What you will be doing:

You will be joining PostHog as a Product Manager, working with one of the small engineering teams (with the potential to cover more than one team/product after onboarding), with a heavy emphasis on analyzing data, talking to users and owning the commercial aspects of your product(s):
- There is a growth review with every product every month. As the PM, you ensure the right metrics exist, dig into things that have changed, understand what users are doing and why they're churning, and use the findings to decide whether to dig deeper into a trend or reprioritize what's being built.
- Across 10+ products with thousands of paying customers and many more on the free tier, finding actionable patterns in this data is key to reaching $100M ARR. Example questions: how does revenue/usage churn for your product compare across different segments? which behaviours lead to long-term retention for your product?
- You keep an eye on the commercial side of things, competing with incumbents multiple times bigger. You lead research into competitors and the tooling landscape and provide recommendations on where the biggest untapped potential is, whether pricing, feature parity, or spinning out an adjacent product.
- Things you choose to pick up: a data deep dive outside the scope of repeating growth reviews, figuring out how to package and price a new product, or rubber-ducking engineers on UX for a new product concept. This is a highly autonomous role; you're expected to figure out where it makes the most sense to spend your time.

What you won't be doing: backlog grooming; deciding or approving what gets built (though you'll help surface context for good decisions); shielding developers from users (instead, invite an engineer to every user interview); project management / writing gazillions of tickets, RFCs, or PRDs; coding and shipping new features yourself (small PRs and hackathon contributions are encouraged).

You'll fit right in if:
- You've been a technical founder or product engineer in a startup -- explicitly looking for someone who has written code before and now wants to focus on the product and commercial side.
- You have strong product sense: you can identify a product's biggest selling points and weaknesses (both user experience and commercial) and turn them into actionable insights.
- You're familiar with business and product metrics (activation, retention, churn) and don't hesitate to write SQL (or another query language) to answer data questions.
- You're very proactive/organized: you figure out what needs to happen, make it happen, and keep multiple threads moving without letting things slip.
- You collaborate well, default to transparency, share early, and seek feedback from the team and customers in an async-first world. Strong communication skills are key.
- You have hands-on experience using observability products and understand how engineers use them to investigate, debug, and diagnose issues in production.

Nice-to-haves (not all required, 2-3 is a big plus): worked on or PM'd an observability product before; additional data modelling experience on top of writing queries; worked on a developer tool or AI product before; done a decent portion of interviewing users already, possibly having worked with designers or engineers on new user experiences.
