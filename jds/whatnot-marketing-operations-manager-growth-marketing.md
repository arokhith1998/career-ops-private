💻 Role

You’ll join the Marketing Strategy & Operations team and own the operational layer behind Whatnot’s paid creative engine: the workflows, reporting, tooling, automation, and vendor infrastructure that let us produce and ship performant paid creative at volume across markets without losing brand fit or signal.

You’ll audit what exists, build a clear point of view on what needs to change, and improve the system end-to-end as paid scales globally.

What You’ll Own

- Process improvement and operating systems. Audit and redesign the operational systems behind Growth Marketing, improving reporting, measurement, tooling, campaign workflows, and decision-making processes to increase speed and effectiveness.
- Creative performance analysis. Analyze creative performance across paid channels to understand what drives business outcomes, identify winning patterns, and partner with Growth Marketing to improve testing strategy. This role focuses on measurement and insights—not campaign trafficking or media buying.
- Cross-functional operating rhythm. Sit at the seam between Performance Marketing, Influencer, and in-house Creative teams. Drive alignment, make tradeoffs visible, and escalate when needed.
- Reporting & creative performance measurement. Own the data infrastructure that powers Growth Marketing (Airtable, Sigma, Snowflake), partnering with Data to build scalable dashboards and reporting frameworks, automate reporting, improve data quality, define how paid creative performance is measured, identify trends across channels, and translate insights into actionable recommendations that drive creative investment, experimentation, and faster decision-making.
- Lead cross-functional operational initiatives end-to-end. Scope the problem, build the plan, drive execution, report results. Be the DRI when a structural problem (localization, brand-fit auditing, attribution across creative variants) doesn't yet have one.
- Net-new problems. As paid scales globally, you'll be the person solving the structural questions nobody's solved yet.

We offer flexibility to work from home or from one of our global office hubs, and we value in-person time for planning, problem-solving, and connection. Team members in this role must live within commuting distance of our SF / LA / NYC hubs, with LA & NYC preferred.

👋 You

People who do well at Whatnot tend to be comfortable figuring things out as they go, biased toward action, and genuinely curious about what they’re building. They care more about outcomes than credit and stay close to the product and the people using it.

For This Role Specifically, You Should Have

- 4+ years of experience within performance marketing & operations at a high-growth consumer company.
- Strong analytical judgment. You’re comfortable working with performance data directly, whether in SQL, BI tools, spreadsheets, ad platforms, or creative analytics tools. You understand paid marketing metrics like CAC, ROAS, LTV, PBP, incrementality, and creative fatigue, and can design measurement frameworks where none exist.
- A track record of building or rebuilding a system that scaled. This could be a tagging taxonomy, asset workflow, creative testing framework, automation pipeline, or reporting system. You’ve shipped something measurable that solved a key business problem, not just managed a process someone else built.
- Strong project management instincts. You’ve run complex cross-functional initiatives across technical and business teams, and you can keep the plan moving without adding unnecessary process.
- Clear written communication. You can take a messy reality and turn it into a one-page recommendation a leader can act on quickly.
- Operational instincts at scale. You’ve worked inside a team where spend, creative volume, or market complexity grew quickly, and you’ve felt the structural pain that comes with it: localization, brand drift, creative velocity, asset tracking, and attribution noise.
- Comfort with ambiguity. You can take a problem like “we don’t know what creative is working globally” and turn it into a practical system the team actually uses.
- Bias toward speed and iteration. You know when a scrappy system is good enough to ship and when something needs more rigor.
