<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>About the Role</strong></p>
<p>Fivetran is building data pipelines to power the modern data stack for thousands of companies.</p>
<p><strong>Every production dbt pipeline depends on orchestration.</strong> When a job runs late, fails without anyone noticing, or gets stuck in a queue, the people downstream stop trusting the data. As Senior Product Manager for Orchestration, you will own how dbt runs in production: how jobs are scheduled and triggered, how they recover when something goes wrong, and how teams find out and fix it.</p>
<p>You will own the vision, roadmap, and outcomes for orchestration on the dbt platform. That covers the job scheduler, triggers and job chaining, retries and run reliability, concurrency and queueing, jobs as code, run monitoring, and notifications. Data teams of every size depend on these systems every day, from a single nightly job to enterprise deployments with hundreds of interdependent jobs across many projects.</p>
<p>You will work closely with the engineering teams behind the scheduler and the v2 engine, with the dbt State team, and with Fivetran’s platform teams as data movement and transformation come together. You will also help define what orchestration looks like as AI agents begin to trigger, monitor, and fix pipelines alongside people.&nbsp;We are looking for someone who is technical, close to customers, and excited by reliability problems: a PM who wants to make the dbt Platform the best place to run dbt in production.</p>
<p>This is a full-time position based out of our <strong>Denver</strong> office. Our hybrid work model offers a blend of remote flexibility and in-person collaboration, including two days in the office each week to connect and build as a team.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>What You’ll Do</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>
<p><strong>Own the orchestration strategy and roadmap</strong></p>
</li>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Define and own the vision and roadmap for dbt platform orchestration, including scheduling, event- and API-based triggers, chaining across jobs and projects, retries, concurrency and queueing, and jobs as code. Turn that vision into clear priorities, sequencing, and tradeoffs.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Set success metrics for run reliability, on-time delivery, and time to recovery, and hold yourself accountable for moving them.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Decide where to invest, what to build, and what not to build, using customer evidence, usage data, and technical judgment.</p>
</li>
</ul>
<li>
<p><strong>Make production dbt reliable and observable</strong></p>
</li>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Partner with engineering to improve run reliability: fewer failed or stuck runs, clearer classification of what went wrong, and faster recovery when it does.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Own notifications and run monitoring across email, Slack, webhooks, and integrations, so teams hear about failures quickly, understand what broke, and know what to do next.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Work with the v2 and dbt State teams to make sure jobs build only what they need to, and stay fast and cost-efficient at scale.</p>
</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Drive cross-functional execution</strong></p>
</li>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Lead engineering and design through a predictable shipping cadence, with crisp feature briefs and PRDs that keep teams aligned on what matters and why.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Partner with Fivetran platform teams on a connected experience for scheduling and running pipelines across data movement and transformation.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Equip Product Marketing, Sales, Solutions Architects, and Support with clear positioning, launch materials, and guidance, including how dbt orchestration compares to standalone orchestrators.</p>
</li>
</ul>
<li>
<p><strong>Stay close to users and the market</strong></p>
</li>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Build tight feedback loops with customers who run dbt in production, from small analytics teams to large enterprises, and with teams that orchestrate dbt from tools like Airflow, Dagster, and Prefect.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Use usage data, support trends, and customer conversations to find the biggest sources of pain, size them, and bring that evidence into planning.</p>
</li>
</ul>
</ul>
